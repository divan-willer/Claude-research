# Follow-up: Validated, rigged biological asset kits: demand, willingness to pay, hidden competitors

> **Method and limits.** I ran 35 WebSearch queries, the full cap. WebFetch was blocked, so I opened no page. Every citation below is a search-result snippet or summary, marked "(snippet)". A few snippets combined several results, so I could not tie a number to one URL. Those are flagged "attribution uncertain". Two queries (Clarafi kit prices; Reddit practitioner threads) returned nothing useful. Today is 7 Nov 2026.

## Bottom line

- **The "missing middle" on price is mostly false for whole-body anatomy.** Mass marketplaces already sell rigged anatomy at mid prices:
  - $149 for a rigged skeletal and circulatory system (CGTrader).
  - $499 for a rigged, animated male anatomy (TurboSquid).
  - $899 for "Ultimate Human Anatomy (Rigged)", with Maya and Blender formats (TurboSquid).
  - $2,399 for a Blender-rigged male and female anatomy set (TurboSquid).
  - TurboSquid also sells an Enhanced Licence tier that adds $10,000 indemnification.

  DOSCH sells a "Human Anatomy V2" pack for $199 and says its models are developed with input from medical experts. VOKA licenses 1,000+ ready-made models (FBX/OBJ/STL) with a commercial-use licence, by quote. **What is still missing** is narrower:
  - documented validation, meaning cited sources and a named reviewer;
  - rigs for organ *function*: heart cycle, peristalsis, diaphragm and lung, bladder filling, uterine motion;
  - a licence that is clear about AI-assisted workflows.
- **The microbe and cell kit gap is weakened on the supply side.** Cheap packs are common:
  - DOSCH: 50 bacteria for EUR 49.
  - ithappy: 190 microorganism models with 38 animations for USD 69, 5 seats.
  - A rigged bacteriophage on TurboSquid, and an animated "Virus Infection Cycle in Host Cell" on Superhive and CGTrader.
  - Free Molecular Nodes with cellPACK covers the molecular and mesoscale layer.

  Only *species-accurate, documented, process-rigged* microbes look unserved, and nobody has shown willingness to pay a premium for that.
- **AI substitutes arrived in 2025-26.**
  - Fundamental XR launched an "Anatomy Inference Engine" beta. It generates parameterised, simulation-ready anatomy with pathology "in minutes rather than weeks".
  - Meshy raised about USD 400M at a USD 1.5B valuation (Jul 2026).
  - BioRender added a "3D" restyle option for icons (Jul 2026) and the Leo AI figure agent. It says it does not offer animation yet, and a 2025 Nature feature reports it is working towards it.

  None of these has been shown to be accurate for biology. All of them cap how much a static asset can be priced.
- **Software use is fragmented, and that hurts a "rigged" kit.**
  - 2026 job postings ask most often for Maya, Blender and Cinema 4D. Houdini is a bonus.
  - A small-studio founder says big medical studios use 3ds Max and Maya, while his studio and others favour Cinema 4D.
  - A rig does not carry across 3D programs, so every target program multiplies rigging cost. Small studios, the likeliest buyers, lean towards Cinema 4D. Large Maya studios already own libraries. (Inference, confidence medium.)
- **The buyer pool is small.**
  - AMI has about 850+ members.
  - No count of studios exists.
  - Blender Market's lifetime payouts average about USD 6.4k per creator.
  - Kit businesses are consolidating and moving to all-you-can-eat pricing. KitBash3D merged with Greyscalegorilla in a 2025 buyout, and its Unlimited plan costs USD 708/yr. Megascans stopped being free after 2024.

  Inference (confidence low-medium): kits alone reach low-to-mid six figures EUR a year at best for a small team, not "massive growth".
- **Microbe-variant demand is real but modest.**
  - WHO counts 90 antibacterials in clinical development (down from 97 in 2023), 40 of them non-traditional (phages, antibodies, microbiome).
  - Vaccines Europe members have 91 vaccine candidates.
  - Phage funding comes in single deals of EUR 6.5M-25M.

  These are hundreds of programmes, each buying animation through a studio. They do not form a direct asset-buying market.
- **Verdicts:**
  - Mid-priced validated rigged anatomy kits: **weakened, survives only as narrowed "organ-function rigs plus validation dossier"**.
  - Rigged cell/microbe MoA kit: **weakened**.
  - Species-level microbe library: **weakened**.
  - Maya bio-toolkit: **unchanged** (no new evidence either way, though the Cinema 4D signal limits it).
  - Outsourced real-time sim anatomy: **weakened** by AI anatomy generators.
  - New, low confidence: **licensing validated biomedical 3D datasets to AI and ed-tech firms**. CGTrader moved AI-training rights into enterprise deals in May 2026, which shows a paid channel exists.

### Q2. Willingness to pay and business-model analogues

**What medical animators already pay for tools** (USD, list prices):
- Clarafi: $360-$600/yr, with kits extra (above).
- Houdini Indie: about $299/yr or $449 for 2 years, for under $100K revenue ([Super Renders Farm 2026](https://superrendersfarm.com/ko/article/what-3d-software-costs-2026); [Shade](https://shade.inc/blog/sidefx-houdini-pricing-2026-fx-core-indie-license-costs)) (snippet; third-party).
- Maya: about $1,945-$2,010/yr (track 4).
- X-Particles: no price found.
- Inference (medium): a solo medical animator's tool budget is a few thousand USD a year. A EUR 300-1,500 kit is an "occasional" purchase, not a subscription line, unless it saves billable days.

**KitBash3D, the closest kit-business analogue.**
- It merged with Greyscalegorilla in Sept 2025, with the co-founders as co-CEOs ([KitBash3D blog](https://kitbash3d.com/a/blog/kitbash3d-greyscalegorilla-merger)) (snippet). Caplight records a buyout/LBO on 9 Sep 2025 led by Eagle Private Capital, amount undisclosed ([Caplight](https://www.caplight.com/company/kitbash3d)) (snippet).
- It claims more than 250,000 artists and studios.
- Commercial subscription is $119/mo or $708/yr (Greyscalegorilla $468/yr; joint $948/yr). It released 480 free assets with Cargo 3.0 ([CG Channel, Apr 2026](https://www.cgchannel.com/2026/04/download-480-free-kitbash-assets-and-materials-with-cargo-3-0/)) (snippet).
- Licence tier is set by company size, and Small Business and Enterprise prices are "inclusive of all employees". More than half of kits were moved to lower prices ([KitBash3D Unlimited](https://kitbash3d.com/pages/kitbash3d-unlimited); [KitBash3D blog](https://kitbash3d.com/a/blog/more-ways-to-build-big-updates-to-our-products-and-plans)) (snippet).

### Q3. Which 3D programs medical animators use

- **Job postings.** Snippets of 2026 job-board postings show Maya, Blender and Cinema 4D asked for most often, often with ZBrush and After Effects. Houdini appears mainly in VFX or real-time roles and as a "bonus". One medical-device freelance post asks for "Cinema 4D + Redshift, Blender, Maya, or similar". Anatomage advertises an in-house "Visual Effects Animation Artist" ([ZipRecruiter](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Medical-Animation/2); [Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/freelance-jobs/apply/Medical-Animator-Motion-Graphics-Designer-Pain-Relief-Niche_~022069620205799550316/); [Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/freelance-jobs/apply/medical-Animator-Surgical-Robotic-System_~022089087164605316754/); [Anatomage via Workable](https://apply.workable.com/anatomage/jobs/view/3EAA11B084.md)) (snippets; aggregated, per-posting attribution uncertain).
- **Practitioner statements.**
  - Cosmocyte's founder: "I know a lot of big medical animation companies use [3ds] Max and Maya but we like Cinema 4D because of its flexibility and easy learning curve" ([Maxon](https://www.maxon.net/en/article/the-art-of-medical-animation); [Maxon](https://www.maxon.net/en/article/expanding-the-reach-of-medical-animation-cg)) (snippet; attribution uncertain between the two Maxon articles).
  - TVASurg (Toronto) runs 20-40-shot videos in Cinema 4D Takes ([TVASurg](https://pie.med.utoronto.ca/TVASurg/tvasurg-process-3d-rendering/)) (snippet).
  - A paid course teaches "Biomedical Animation … with Cinema 4D" ([Cinematic Science](https://cinematic-science.teachable.com/l/pdp/biomedical-animation-with-cinema-4d)) (snippet).
  - Toronto BMC theses use ZBrush, then Maya, then After Effects ([example](https://sites.google.com/site/cfmatuk/Projects/the-plastic-brain)) [pre-2024].
  - Garagefarm notes medical work "is less likely to need sophisticated character controls", so MASH and MoGraph matter more ([Garagefarm](https://garagefarm.net/blog/medical-visualization-iii-software-and-workflow)) (snippet).
- **Surveys.** I found no biomedical-specific software survey. General surveys only: Poliigon State of CG 2024 (n=6,251) ([Poliigon](https://poliigon.com/articles/state-of-cg-2024)) (snippet).
- Inference (medium):
  - A *rig* is tied to one program. Maya rigs (mGear, custom deformers) serve large studios and Maya freelancers. Cinema 4D studios, which are many small buyers, need either Cinema 4D-native rigs or baked caches (Alembic/USD).
  - Blender users get free molecular tools.
  - A credible format plan is: Maya-native rigs as the premium tier; Alembic/USD/FBX baked motion libraries for Cinema 4D, Blender and Houdini; and possibly Cinema 4D-native rigs later.
  - The garagefarm point also suggests most medical motion is motion-graphics and deformer-driven, not character-rigged. That lowers the value of a "rigged" label unless the rig drives organ function.

  - Sellers complained that licences were switched to AI-permitting automatically ([CGTrader forum](https://www.cgtrader.com/forum/site-suggestions-and-feedback/turning-standard-licenses-into-ai-license-automatically-really); [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/3d-model-marketplace-cgtrader-accused-of-being-evil-for-pushing-ai)) (snippet).
  - In 2025 TurboSquid licensed assets for AI training under an opt-out, and Cubebrush banned AI-generated content ([CG Channel, May 2025](https://www.cgchannel.com/2025/05/cubebrush-bans-ai-generated-content-from-its-online-marketplace/)) (snippet).
  - CGTrader sells "3D Model Datasets for Next-Gen AI" ([CGTrader datasets](https://admin.cgtrader.com/datasets)) (snippet).
  - Inference (medium): marketplace licence terms are now unstable and mixed. A clear licence written for pharma use, covering commercial use, AI-*assisted* production allowed, no redistribution or training, and provenance, is a real differentiator. But TurboSquid's $10k indemnification tier already exists as a "safe licence" option.

### Q5. Microbe variant

- **Antibacterials.** WHO's 2025 analysis (data to 15 Feb 2025) counts 90 antibacterial agents in clinical development: 50 traditional and 40 non-traditional (phages, antibodies, microbiome-modulating). That is down from 97 in 2023. Only 15 are innovative. There are 232 preclinical programmes across 148 groups, mostly small firms ([WHO](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240113091); [WHO news, 2 Oct 2025](https://www.who.int/news/item/02-10-2025-who-releases-new-reports-on-new-tests-and-treatments-in-development-for-bacterial-infections); [WHO observatory](https://www.who.int/observatories/global-observatory-on-health-research-and-development/monitoring/antibacterial-products-in-clinical-development-for-priority-pathogens)) (snippet).
- **Vaccines.**
  - Vaccines Europe Pipeline Review (1 Dec 2025): 91 candidates (86 prophylactic vaccines, 2 therapeutic, 3 prophylactic mAbs). 41% target pathogens with no registered vaccine ([Vaccines Europe](https://www.vaccineseurope.eu/media-hub/press-releases/91-innovative-vaccines-in-development-by-vaccines-europe-member-companies/)) (snippet).
  - Policy Cures: 1,418 active candidates (drugs, vaccines, biologics, diagnostics) for neglected and emerging infections, as of Feb 2025 ([Policy Cures](https://policycuresresearch.org/pipeline-database)) (snippet).
  - A BIO-derived count fell from 560 products (2025) to 308 (2026), partly because of method changes ([IZ Summit summary](https://www.izsummitpartners.org/content/uploads/2026_05_07_swu_summary.pdf)) (snippet; attribution uncertain).
- **Phage and microbiome funding** (single deals, no aggregate found):
  - Phagos EUR 25M Series A, Oct 2025, veterinary focus ([Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2025/10/08/phagos-gets-eur25m-boost-to-combat-bacterial-diseases/)) (snippet).
  - SNIPR Biome up to EUR 6.5M from the CF Foundation, Sep 2025 ([CFF](https://www.cff.org/news/2025-09/cf-foundation-invests-65-million-euros-snipr-biome-phages)) (snippet).
  - Microbiotix KRW 20bn Series B ([BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/microbiotix-finalizes-krw-20-billion-series-b-securing-koreas-first-bacteriophage-clinical-trial)) (snippet).
  - CARB-X grants for phage work ([CARB-X](https://carb-x.org/carb-x-news/carb-x-to-support-phiogens-phage-based-project-targeting-e-coli-infections/)) (snippet).
- **Existing packs and prices:** DOSCH EUR 49 for 50 bacteria; ithappy $69 for 190 models; CGTrader $18 virus/cell pack; Unity virus packs; a rigged bacteriophage; a virus-infection-cycle asset (all above).
- **Not found:** AMR campaign budgets and microbiology course enrolment.
- Inference (low-medium): a few hundred anti-infective and vaccine programmes buy MoA animation through studios. Demand for species-accurate microbes is real, but it is *derived* demand. One studio-level licence covers many programmes. The ceiling is set by the number of studios, not the number of programmes.

### Conclusion: does the gap survive?

**Partly, in a narrower form.** The broad claim that "nobody sells mid-priced, rigged, licensed biological assets" fails.

The part that survives:
- **Organ-function and cell-process rigs**, such as cardiac cycle, peristalsis, diaphragm and lung, bladder, uterus and pelvic floor, phagocytosis and viral entry.
- **A validation dossier** for each asset: sources, reviewer and version.
- **A licence written for pharma and agency use.**
- **Formats** for Maya (native rigs) plus Alembic/USD caches for Cinema 4D, Blender and Houdini.

I found no listing that combines these. Inference, confidence medium.

**Who buys first** (inference, confidence low-medium):
1. Freelance medical animators and 2-20-person studios that lack internal libraries. They are reachable through TurboSquid, CGTrader, Fab and Superhive, and directly.
2. Small agency in-house 3D teams.
3. Later, ed-tech, simulation and AI firms licensing datasets.

**At what price** (inference, low-medium):
- EUR 99-499 per kit for an individual seat. This is anchored by marketplace rigged anatomy at $149-$899, DOSCH at $199 and ithappy at $69.
- EUR 1,000-3,000 for a studio licence of a themed collection with a validation dossier.
- EUR 5,000+ only with custom rigging or adaptation work bundled in.

**How big** (inference, low): I found no count of studios. Proxies:
- AMI has 850+ members.
- Listicles name about 15 "top" studios per country.

A plausible paying pool is 1,000-3,000 studios, freelancers and in-house teams worldwide.
- At EUR 300-1,500 a year, the theoretical market is about EUR 0.3M-4.5M a year.
- A small team realistically captures EUR 50k-300k a year within 2-3 years.
- Ed-tech, simulation and AI-dataset licences could add EUR 50k-500k a year, but there is no price evidence.

That is a credible first-revenue line and a portfolio and lead engine for custom rigging work. On this evidence it is not a "massive growth" business by itself.

## Evidence that cuts AGAINST the candidate gaps

1. **Mid-priced rigged anatomy already exists.** It sells for $149-$2,399 on TurboSquid and CGTrader, in Maya and Blender formats. The "missing middle" holds only for *validated, function-rigged* assets (sources above).
2. **"Pharma-safe licence" is not unique.** TurboSquid sells an Enhanced Licence with $10,000 indemnification. VOKA states a commercial-use licence on 1,000+ models.
3. **Prices for "medically informed" packs are very low.** DOSCH's anatomy at $199 and bacteria at EUR 49 for 50 models, plus ithappy at $69 for 190 models (often 30-70% off), set buyer expectations near EUR 1 per model.
4. **Process-level micro assets exist.** A rigged bacteriophage, a virus-infection-cycle asset, and Unity packs of 150-350 viruses are on sale. Molecular Nodes with cellPACK is free and active (Mar 2026).
5. **Established studios reuse and generate their own assets.** Elipse, Maverick Frame, Innowise, The Danse and VOKA all say so, and AXS models tissues procedurally. I found no first-hand "we rebuild every project" testimony.
6. **Software fragmentation multiplies the cost of a rigged kit.** Cinema 4D is favoured by small studios and Maya and 3ds Max by big ones (Cosmocyte quote; job postings). Big Maya studios own libraries; small Cinema 4D studios need baked caches, not Maya rigs.
7. **AI substitutes are arriving with heavy funding.** Fundamental XR's AIE generates parameterised anatomy with pathology. Meshy raised $1.5B-valuation capital. BioRender added a 3D style and the Leo agent, and is working towards animation.
8. **The buyer pool is small and marketplace earnings are long-tail.** AMI has about 850+ members. Blender Market averages about $6.4k per creator lifetime, and about $385 per paid creator in one sale.
9. **The kit-business analogue is under pressure.** KitBash3D merged with Greyscalegorilla in a 2025 buyout, moved to an all-inclusive $708/yr plan, cut prices on more than half its kits, and gives away 480 assets. Megascans stopped being free and kept 1,500+ assets free. Customers now expect all-you-can-eat pricing.
10. **The microbe pipeline is modest and shrinking in places.** WHO counts 90 clinical antibacterials, down from 97. A BIO-derived count fell from 560 to 308, partly from method changes. Phage rounds are EUR 6.5M-25M.
11. **Ed-tech and simulation platforms build in-house.** Anatomage advertises an in-house VFX animation artist role, and VOKA and SciePro own large libraries.

## Still unknown

- The **number of medical animation studios worldwide**, and how many lack internal libraries. No census or directory count was found.
- **Sales volumes** of medical and science assets on TurboSquid, CGTrader, Fab and Superhive, and what the bestsellers are.
- Prices for **Clarafi kits, current Zygote collections, VOKA licences, SciePro rigged models** (and how many SciePro models are rigged), the **Fab microorganism pack, X-Particles**, and Fab Megascans per-asset prices.
- Whether buyers **pay extra for a validation dossier**. There is no buyer interview, survey or forum evidence. This is the critical untested assumption.
- **Practitioner forum voice.** No Reddit, LinkedIn or AMI-forum threads were retrievable.
- Fundamental XR AIE **launch date, pricing, output accuracy and whether it covers organs or cells**. Whether BioRender plans **true 3D assets** or animation export.
- **Adobe Substance 3D healthcare assets**: scope and licence (title-only lead).
- **Ed-tech, simulation and AI-dataset licence prices** for biomedical 3D (no comparables).
- **AMR campaign budgets, microbiology enrolment**, and an aggregate for microbiome and phage funding in 2025.
- A biomedical-specific **software-use survey** (AMI or BMC programmes).
