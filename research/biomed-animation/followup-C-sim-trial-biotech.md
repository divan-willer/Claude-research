# Follow-up: Simulation anatomy licensing, trial-participant content, and biotech MoA packages

Follow-up researcher C. Date: 7 November 2026. Method: 35 WebSearch queries (the hard cap). WebFetch is blocked in this environment, so I opened no page. Every fact below comes from a search-result summary or snippet and is marked "(snippet)". Where a summary merged several results and I could not tie a number to one URL, I say "attribution uncertain". I did not repeat searches the six track reports or follow-ups A and B already ran (SciePro/Zygote/marketplace prices, Osso's 2024 layoff, the JAMA RCT's design, VarsaAI/Powtoon). Surgical Science and Mentice both report Q3 2026 after today (12 and 10 Nov 2026), so their latest public figures are H1 2026.

## Bottom line

- **The public simulation companies are not a growing, outsourcing buyer pool.** Surgical Science is the largest listed surgical-simulation firm, with 2025 net sales of SEK 992.3m. Its comparable-unit growth was only 4%, operating profit roughly halved, and it lost a planned Intuitive da Vinci 5 deal. Intuitive now ships its own basic simulation package and keeps adding exercises to SimNow 2. Mentice's 2025 net sales fell 3.9% to SEK 279.1m, and its EBITDA was SEK 3.4m. Osso VR has pivoted to nurse training. These are signs of OEMs bringing simulation in-house and of thin margins, not of a market that buys anatomy from outside.
- **The medtech-industry demand signal is real, but it goes to sim vendors, not to anatomy suppliers.** 89% of Mentice's 2025 order intake (SEK 253.9m of 285.1m) came from medical-device companies, up 9.6%. Its H1 2026 orders rose 36%. J&J's Institute promoted VR customer training in May 2026. Level Ex (owned by Brainlab) is funded by device makers and pharma companies whose products appear in its games. Device makers pay for training simulations. I found no evidence that they or the sim vendors license third-party anatomy.
- **Simulation companies build anatomy in-house. Nobody visibly licenses it.** In 2024-2026 I found no deal in which a sim or XR firm licensed anatomy from BioDigital, Zygote or Primal; the only examples are from 2018-2022. BioDigital's SDK and API tiers are priced by quote only. Sim-adjacent firms hire their own 3D anatomy artists: Labster/UbiSim, Anatomage (staff roles, and also a remote contract role), Arthrex and Level Ex. **Verdict, Gap 1 (sim anatomy licensing): weakened as a licensing business. It survives only as contract or overflow art work** for XR and nursing-sim developers and medtech education teams. Confidence: medium.
- **Trial-participant content has an established incumbent that is moving away from content.** Jumo Health has private-equity backing (Falfurrias, Aug 2024) and a new CEO (2025). Its revenue is estimated at about USD 37m, with about 100 staff (aggregator estimates, weak). It is repositioning around PRISM, a recruitment and retention "system", with an AI partner (Areti, Feb 2026). CISCRP and Citeline sell plain-language summaries. eConsent vendors keep multimedia in-house or co-market with Jumo (Medable). I found no published price for any of this. **Verdict, Gap 2: survives only as white-label subcontracting** (3D anatomy and mechanism-of-disease modules supplied to Jumo-type firms and eConsent creative teams). It is not viable as a direct-to-sponsor business for this founder within 12 months. Confidence: low-medium.
- **The evidence that animation helps consent is still mixed.** The 2026 JAMA RCT (n=1,535) found no gain over text, but it tested consent to join a research cohort. A Feb 2026 conference meta-analysis abstract found that video or computer consent improves knowledge (OR 2.21), with high heterogeneity (I² 87%). Free substitutes exist: ECRAN's CC-licensed animated trial explainer is available in 23 languages.
- **Biotech financing recovered in 2026, but early-stage money is lagging.** Counts of US biotech IPOs differ by source, but most give about 20-27 through Q3 2026, against 8 in all of 2025: 18 in H1 (BioSpace), plus 9 in Q3 (Renaissance), with Lycia's USD 317m IPO on 23 Oct. Biopharma venture funding reached USD 23.9bn through 30 Sept (J.P. Morgan/DealForma). However, Q3 fell 32% from Q2, and BioCentury reports that seed and Series A rounds lagged the recovery. So the companies with money are later-stage and IPO-bound.
- **Biotechs do buy MoA animation for corporate and investor use, but a "fast, fixed-price" offer barely differentiates.** Visual Science lists corporate-presentation and investor-relations projects for Apogee, Jasper, Spyre, Diakonos, Protara and ForeBio. Studios already deliver in 5-9 weeks. VOKA quotes from USD 7,000, or USD 10-20k per finished minute. Freelancers charge USD 1.5-4k per minute, and Fiverr MoA gigs exist. I found no studio publishing a fixed-price investor package. **Verdict, Gap 3: survives as the most realistic first-revenue path.** The edge has to come from 3D quality at a mid-tier price plus a deck-ready format (a 60-90 s loop, stills, 2D schematic layer), not from speed. First buyers: Series B-C and newly-IPO'd biotechs. Rough price: USD 8-20k per 60-90 s piece (inference). Confidence: medium.
- **What changed versus the track reports:**
  - Track 4's sim/XR outsourcing gap loses its main premise: buyers are insourcing (Intuitive) or pivoting (Osso).
  - Track 1's IPO count conflict is resolved to about 20-27 US biotech IPOs through Q3 2026. BioSpace's 31 and Sleuth's 43 use wider scopes.
  - Track 6's trial-content gap gains an incumbent profile (Jumo's scale and its pivot) but still has no price evidence.

  - recruitment problems account for 57% of failed Phase 2 and 54% of failed Phase 3 trials (Phesi, via [Pharmaceutical Executive](https://pharmexec.com/view/clinical-trials-dont-have-cost-too-much-or-take-too-long), snippet; date not shown);
  - McKinsey: participants enrolled per site per month in Phase 3 fell 14% (oncology) and 54% (non-oncology) between 2012-14 and 2021-23 ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/life-sciences/our-insights/accelerating-clinical-trials-to-improve-biopharma-r-and-d-productivity.), snippet).
- Inference (confidence medium): the money sits in recruitment and retention services, where content is one input. Content-only vendors capture a small share.

**Evidence on animation and video in consent (2024-2026)**
- JAMA Network Open RCT (Gouda et al., 1 Apr 2026, n=1,535, four arms): no gain in consent or comprehension from animated, physician or patient video over text, in a virtual research-cohort consent. Track 6 round 2 corroborated this ([JAMA Netw Open](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2848407); [Duke](https://comphealth.duke.edu/publications/audiovisual-augmentation-of-electronic-consent-to-improve-consent-rates-and-comprehension-a-randomized-clinical-trial/), snippets). I did not re-search it.
- A Feb 2026 conference abstract of a systematic review and meta-analysis found that video- and computer-based consent improved knowledge versus conventional consent (OR 2.21) with no added anxiety, but heterogeneity was high (I² = 87%). It is a poster, not a full paper ([PMC12901548](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12901548/), snippet).
- Older reviews lean positive and stress that design matters: Gesualdo 2021 in BMC Medical Ethics ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/s12910-021-00585-8.pdf)), and a 2023 BJS Open review of RCTs on digital surgical consent ([PMC9874030](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9874030)) (snippets) [pre-2024]. The kidney-biopsy RCT (CJASN 2025) is positive (track 6).
- Free substitute: ECRAN's 5-minute animated "introduction to clinical trials" is available in 23 languages under Creative Commons ([Testing Treatments](https://en.testingtreatments.org/?p=3055); [PMC4710012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4710012), snippets) [pre-2024].
- Inference (confidence medium): animation helps most for procedure-heavy or complex interventions and for children. Generic "what is a trial" content is commoditised and free.

## Evidence that cuts AGAINST the candidate gaps

**Gap 1 (sim anatomy licensing/outsourcing)**
- The largest robot OEM is insourcing basic simulation. Intuitive dropped the plan to ship Surgical Science software on every da Vinci 5, and Surgical Science guided to a SEK 60-90m licence-revenue hit ([Investegate](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/mfn/surgical-science-sweden-ab--0aau/year-end-report-2025-profitability-in-line-w-/9437110); [Intuitive](https://investor.intuitivesurgical.com/news-releases/news-release-details/intuitive-announces-innovations-advance-quintuple-aim), snippets).
- The listed sim vendors are low-growth or low-margin:
  - Surgical Science: 4% comparable growth, operating profit down 52%;
  - Mentice: sales −3.9%, EBITDA SEK 3.4m on SEK 279m of sales.
- Osso VR is pivoting to nurse onboarding rather than expanding medtech surgical content.
- No 2024-26 licensing deal between an anatomy library and a sim firm surfaced. The known examples are from 2018-2022.
- Sim-adjacent firms hire anatomy artists in-house: Anatomage, Labster/UbiSim, Arthrex, Level Ex.
- AI anatomy generation for simulation has arrived: Fundamental XR's Anatomy Inference Engine (follow-up A).

**Gap 2 (trial-participant content)**
- The incumbent (Jumo) is moving to a recruitment "system" with an AI partner. Lay-summary vendors are automating text with AI (TrialAssure with MMS).
- eConsent vendors build in-house (Signant) or partner with Jumo (Medable). Veeva's site eConsent is free.
- Evidence that animation helps is mixed: the JAMA RCT was null, and the meta-analysis abstract has I² = 87%.
- A free 23-language animated trial explainer exists (ECRAN).
- No price evidence exists anywhere.

**Gap 3 (biotech MoA packages)**
- Quarterly biotech venture funding fell 32% in Q3 2026, and seed and Series A rounds lag. The cheapest, fastest-moving buyers are the weakest segment.
- Turnaround is not a differentiator: Visual Science already delivers in 5-6 weeks.
- Price competition comes from three directions: VOKA from USD 7k, freelancers at USD 1.5-4k/min and Fiverr gigs, and AI-MoA tools (VarsaAI, Powtoon; follow-up B).
- Biotech IPO stock performance lagged the index by late October (Renaissance IPO Index +6% vs S&P +13%). If the window narrows, demand falls fast.
