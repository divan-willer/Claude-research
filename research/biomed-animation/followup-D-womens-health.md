# Follow-up: Women's health / female pelvic anatomy as a vertical

> **Method and limits.** I ran 30 WebSearch queries, the full cap. WebFetch was blocked, so I opened no page. Every citation is a search-result snippet or summary, marked "(snippet)". Where a summary merged several results and I could not tie a number to one URL, I say "attribution uncertain". I read tracks 2, 4 and 6 and follow-up A first and did not repeat their searches. Their figures are reused here only with their original URLs. Today is 7 Nov 2026. Sources dated before 2024 are flagged [pre-2024, possibly stale].

## Bottom line

- **Demand is real but narrow.** Between 2024 and 2026 women's health got one new menopause drug, Bayer's Lynkuet (FDA approval Oct 2025, US launch planned Nov 2025). It also got three oral antibiotics for uncomplicated UTI in women (Pivya, Orlynvah, Blujepa) and a growing PPD drug (Zurzuvae, $46.4M in Q2 2025). I found **no new 2024–26 approvals in endometriosis, fibroids, PCOS or contraception**. Organon's women's-health revenue was flat at $1.75B in 2025, with US Nexplanon sales down 9% under policy restrictions. CooperSurgical sells $1.35B a year in office/surgical and fertility products.
- **Funding signals conflict.** SVB says women's-health venture funding fell from $3.2B (2024) to about $2B (2025), and women's-health biopharma fell from $1.3B to $610M. W Group counts a record $1.55B of disclosed equity in 2025. The pelvic-floor and continence device startups I found raised $3M–$45M each.
- **There are not many fertility clinics, and their education market is already taken.** The US has about 450–560 ART clinics. Private-equity groups were affiliated with 32% of IVF clinics in 2023, and those clinics perform more than half of US IVF cycles. EngagedMD serves 250+ fertility clinics and signed IVI RMA in Jan 2026. ViewMedica already sells OB/GYN (56 modules) and fertility libraries from $85/month or $1,020/year.
- **The supply gap in the big platforms is closing.** Complete Anatomy shipped a full female model in 2022. Visible Body added uterus and pregnancy models in May 2025. BioDigital launched a 40-model **Women's Health collection for patient education (incl. menopause and pelvic floor) in Feb 2026** and added gynaecology procedures in July 2026. Published critiques of male-default anatomy are mostly about *textbooks*, and mostly from before 2024.
- **The gap that still holds is narrow.** I found no *validated, function-rigged, exportable* female pelvis: pelvic-floor contraction and descent, bladder filling and voiding, prolapse grades, device placement. Zygote's rig add-on is male-only (Maya only). SciePro charges from €29,900 per model. Marketplace female reproductive models sell for $12–$299, and their "rigged" tags are supplied by the sellers. Finite-element research on how the pelvic floor moves in childbirth exists, but nobody has turned it into animation-ready assets.
- **Who pays first (inference, confidence low-medium):**
  1. Women's-health device startups and mid-size device makers (incontinence, prolapse, pelvic floor, contraception, fertility devices). They commission mode-of-delivery (MoD) and procedure animations at about **$3k–$25k per piece**. VOKA sells ready-made gynaecology clips from $500 and custom work from $7,000. Advids sells mostly-2D femtech explainers at $1.2k–$2k.
  2. Medical animation studios and litigation-graphics firms (birth injury, urogynaecology). They would buy a female pelvic kit at about **$300–$3,000**.
- **Size (inference, low confidence).** As a niche studio plus kit business it is worth perhaps $0.1M–$0.3M in year 1. A clinic patient-education play has a ceiling of roughly $0.5M–$1.7M a year in the US at ViewMedica-like prices, and incumbents already hold it.
- **Verdict: a sharpener, not a standalone market.** Women's health makes **Gap 1 (validated rigged asset kits)** credible as a beachhead: "the validated, function-rigged female pelvis". It also gives a device-MoD service a clear first customer. It does **not** create a large new market on its own, and patient education for fertility and OB/GYN clinics is the weakest of the three routes.

### Q2. Supply: is female anatomy under-represented or inaccurate?

**Published critiques (mostly textbooks, mostly pre-2024)**
- O'Connell et al. (2005) found most anatomy textbooks omitted or misdescribed the clitoris while covering the penis in detail ([ASU Embryo Project](https://embryo.asu.edu/pages/anatomy-clitoris-2005-helen-e-oconnell-kalavampara-v-sanjeevan-and-john-m-hutson)) (snippet) [pre-2024].
- Beni, Fisher and Longhurst (2022 letter) analysed 32 textbooks from 2007–2021. "Penis" appeared 2.6 times as often as "clitoris", and only one book used a photo of a woman of colour ([SGUL open access](https://openaccess.sgul.ac.uk/114401/1/Anatomical%20Sciences%20Ed%20-%202022%20-%20Beni%20-%20The%20importance%20of%20diverse%20and%20accurate%20descriptions%20of%20genital%20anatomy%20in%20textbooks.pdf)) (snippet) [pre-2024].
- Hayes and Temple-Smith (2022) found no textbook covered normal variation in vulval appearance, and *Last's Anatomy* has no vulva image ([University of Melbourne](https://research.unimelb.edu.au/strengths/updates/discovery/anatomy-textbooks-dont-address-the-diversity-in-appearance-of-female-genitalia); [PMC9541205](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9541205)) (snippet) [pre-2024].
- Image share:
  - Darici et al.: German anatomy books show about 30% female images, against 66% on e-learning platforms ([Bohrium/arXiv record](https://bohrium.dp.tech/paper/arxiv/35844161)) (snippet, date unclear).
  - A thesis on about 6,000 Australian textbook images found females are 36% of all images ([CORE](https://core.ac.uk/download/81228207.pdf)) (snippet, date unclear).
- UMass Chan anatomist Yasmin Carter: male-based digital models "have been used as unisex" and miss female differences such as the elbow carrying angle ([UMass](https://www.massachusetts.edu/news/umass-chan-anatomist-involved-design-3d-female-anatomy-teaching-model)) (snippet, date not shown).
- Students reported that female external genitalia were taught in less detail than male ([Lancaster ePrints](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/236559/)) (snippet, date not shown).
- I found **no peer-reviewed study that measures errors in today's 3D platforms**. Evidence thin.

### Conclusion: does women's health sharpen a candidate gap?

| Candidate gap | Effect of women's-health lens | Why | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gap 1: validated rigged asset kits | **Sharpens most.** It gives the kit line a first SKU and a story | Zygote's rig is male-only. SciePro is €29.9k. Marketplace female models are cheap but unvalidated. Viewer platforms (BioDigital, Complete Anatomy) are not exportable rigs. The founder already owns the sculpt | Medium (supply); low (willingness to pay) |
| Device MoD / procedure animation (service) | **Sharpens.** It gives a clear first customer type | A steady flow of funded pelvic-health, continence and prolapse device startups ($3M–$45M rounds). CooperSurgical office/surgical is $824M a year. The kit cuts the studio's cost per job | Medium-low |
| Pharma MoA | **Weak.** There are few launches and the brands are concentrated | One big menopause launch (Lynkuet) and three uUTI antibiotics in 2024–26. Big pharma uses agency-of-record studios. I did not observe who made the Lynkuet or Veozah MoA films | Low |
| Patient education (fertility, OB/GYN, menopause, pelvic floor) | **Does not sharpen.** It is crowded and cheap | ViewMedica $85/month to $1,020/year. BioDigital's Women's Health collection (Feb 2026). EngagedMD 250+ clinics plus IVI RMA. Clinics build in-house (CCRM) | Medium |

- **Who pays first (inference, low-medium):**
  1. Seed- to Series-B women's-health device companies, at $3k–$25k per MoD or procedure animation, where founders decide fast.
  2. Medical-animation studios and litigation-graphics firms, at $300–$3,000 per female pelvic kit.
  3. Later, licensing the rigged female pelvis into patient-education platforms (ViewMedica, EngagedMD or a fertility network) rather than selling to clinics one by one.
- **How big (inference, low):**
  - US clinic patient education: about 450–560 clinics × $1k–$3k a year ≈ $0.5M–$1.7M a year, mostly held by incumbents.
  - Device MoD: perhaps 20–40 funded women's-health device firms a year worldwide × 1–3 animations × $5k–$25k ≈ $0.1M–$3M a year.
  - Kits: hundreds of units a year at most.
  - A small team could plausibly reach $100k–$300k in year 1. The vertical alone does not support "massive growth". Growth would come from using the female pelvis as the flagship of a broader validated, function-rigged anatomy library.

## Evidence against

- **Incumbents are closing the female-anatomy gap now.** BioDigital launched a Women's Health patient-education collection with menopause and pelvic-floor content in Feb 2026 and gynaecology procedures in Jul 2026 ([BioDigital](https://support.biodigital.com/hc/en-us/articles/38618734866583-February-2026-New-Expert-Collection-Women-s-Health-for-Patient-Education)). Visible Body added uterus and pregnancy models in May 2025. Complete Anatomy has had a full female model since 2022.
- **Cheap female pelvic geometry is everywhere.** Marketplace models sell for $12–$299, some tagged rigged, and several are "female reproductive and urinary system" listings, the same subject as the founder's asset.
- **The critiques are dated and aimed at textbooks.** They are not about the 3D production assets studios buy. I found no study showing errors in current 3D platforms.
- **Few drug launches.** I found no new 2024–26 approvals in endometriosis, fibroids, PCOS or contraception. The menopause class has two brands (Veozah, Lynkuet), each of which will already have agency MoA content.
- **Policy and revenue headwinds.** Organon's women's health revenue fell 1% in 2025, and US Nexplanon sales fell 9% under policy restrictions ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260212249326/en/Organon-Reports-Results-for-the-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-Ended-December-31-2025)).
- **Funding fell in 2025 by two of three measures.** SVB: about $2B, with biopharma down to $610M. Deloitte (attribution uncertain): down 56% in health tech.
- **Fertility education is locked up.** EngagedMD serves 250+ clinics and signed IVI RMA. ViewMedica is cheap. Big networks build in-house (CCRM). Most cycles now run through PE-backed networks with central procurement.
- **The animation-versus-text question (from track 6).** A 1,535-person JAMA Netw Open RCT found animated eConsent no better than text ([JAMA Netw Open](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2848407)). That weakens the case for premium animated patient education.
- **Studios already do gynaecology.** VOKA already markets gynaecology and obstetric animation with a ready-made library from $500, so device buyers have a cheap option.

- Availability and cost of a urogynaecologist or OB-GYN validation partner for the founder's assets.
- Accuracy of the founder's own model against FE and MRI literature (needs expert review).
