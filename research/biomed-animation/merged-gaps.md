## Intro

This file merges every candidate gap the team proposed. I applied the round-2 fact-checks. CONTRADICTED values replace the originals. SAME-SOURCE ONLY and vendor-only claims count as weak. All evidence is at search-snippet level, because no page was ever opened.

Tags in the evidence lines:
- **[C]** corroborated by an independent source in fact-check round 2.
- **[S]** single source, or SAME-SOURCE ONLY. Every follow-up fact is [S] unless a track check also found it, because the follow-ups were never fact-checked.
- **[V]** vendor or company self-claim.
- **[U]** NOT FOUND or unverified in round 2.
- **[I]** inference.

**Headline.**
- Nine gaps survive: seven standalone gaps and two hybrids. Most are narrowed versions of round-1 ideas. Nineteen ideas, or broad versions of ideas, are discarded or folded into a survivor.
- The best overall play is **G8**, a kit-backed MoA/MoD studio. Cash comes from G2 investor MoA packs and G3 women's-health device MoD work. Each job adds a validated, function-rigged asset to the G1 library.

## Surviving gaps

### G1. Validated, function-rigged organ and cell-process kits (female pelvis first)

- **Kind:** product.
- **One-liner:** Organ-function and cell-process rigs, each with a validation dossier and a pharma-safe licence. Maya-native rigs plus Alembic/USD caches for Cinema 4D, Blender and Houdini.
- **Narrowed from:** track 1 Gap 3, track 2 Gap 2, track 3 Gap 1, track 4 Gaps 1-2, track 5 Gap 1, track 6 Gap 2, follow-ups A and D. **Cut:**
  - "Rigged anatomy at a mid price": it already exists.
  - Static microbe packs: cheap.
  - "BioRender of 3D" as a broad library.
  - A safe licence as a unique selling point: TurboSquid sells USD 10k indemnification.
  - A standalone provenance product: its residue is the dossier.
- **Problem:** Cheap rigged anatomy is everywhere, but the rigs are skeletal or joint rigs, or baked hearts and lungs. None cites sources or a reviewer. Organ function is improvised: a contracting heart, peristalsis, diaphragm and lung, bladder filling and voiding, pelvic-floor descent, phagocytosis, viral entry. Small studios and freelancers have no internal library to fall back on.
- **Buyer:** Freelance medical animators and studios of 2-20 people. Small agency in-house 3D teams. Later, litigation-graphics firms (birth injury, urogynaecology) and ed-tech teams.
- **Evidence:**
  - Mid-priced rigged anatomy exists, with no validation shown. [C] (found independently in track 4 r2 and follow-up A.) Examples:
    - CGTrader rigged M/F collection, USD 518.70 ([link](https://cgtrader.com/3d-models/character/anatomy/collection-rigged-human-male-and-female-anatomy)).
    - TurboSquid rigged male anatomy, USD 499 ([link](https://www.turbosquid.com/3d-models/3d-male-anatomy-rigged-animated-2577491)).
    - TurboSquid Blender-rigged M/F set, USD 2,399 ([link](https://www.turbosquid.com/3d-models/male-female-anatomy-set-3d-model-1594415)).
  - Organ motion on marketplaces is baked hearts and lungs at USD 15-299 ([CGTrader](https://www.cgtrader.com/3d-models/science/medical/human-heart-rigged-and-animated)) [S]. One lung animation runs only in 3ds Max, per a buyer review [S].
  - Houdini users improvise a beating heart: "I claim no physical accuracy" ([SideFX](https://sidefx.com/forum/topic/96072)) [S].
  - No validated, provenance-documented rigged library was found (track 5 r2 row 10; follow-up A) [I, medium].
  - Zygote's rig is a male-only Maya add-on ([Zygote](https://www.zygote.com/poly-models/3d-human-collections/3d-premier-anatomy-collection)) [U]. SciePro starts at EUR 29,900 per model ([SciePro](https://www.sciepro.com/pricing)) [U].
  - Female pelvic models sell for USD 12-299. Sellers tag them "rigged", and none claims validation ([CGTrader](https://www.cgtrader.com/3d-models/science/medical/female-repruductive-system-set)) [S].
  - Viewer platforms added female content, but you cannot export their rigs ([BioDigital Feb 2026](https://support.biodigital.com/hc/en-us/articles/38618734866583-February-2026-New-Expert-Collection-Women-s-Health-for-Patient-Education)) [S].
  - Licence terms are unstable. CGTrader moved AI-training rights into enterprise deals on 15 May 2026 ([CGTrader](https://help.cgtrader.com/hc/en-us/articles/46191349842705-Can-my-models-still-be-licensed-for-AI-training)) [S]. Its "No AI" licence is a standard option [C], so licensing alone is a weak pain.
  - Established studios reuse in-house, so the pain is with small buyers ([Maverick Frame](https://maverickframe.com/services/medical-animation/); [VOKA](https://voka.io/3d-scientific-animation-services/molecular-biology/)) [V].
- **Counter-evidence:**
  - Nobody has tested willingness to pay for a validation dossier. There is no interview, survey or forum evidence (follow-up A).
  - Low price anchors: DOSCH Human Anatomy V2 at USD 199 ([link](https://www.cgtrader.com/3d-models/character/anatomy/dosch-3d-human-anatomy-v2)), and a TurboSquid USD 10k indemnification tier ([link](https://www.turbosquid.com/3d-model/enhanced-licenses/anatomy/rig/gltf)) [S].
  - Small studios favour Cinema 4D ([Maxon/Cosmocyte](https://www.maxon.net/en/article/the-art-of-medical-animation)) [S]. Rigs do not carry across programs.
  - The pool is small: AMI has about 850 members. Blender Market pays about USD 6.4k per creator over their lifetime ([stats](https://BlenderMarket.com/market-stats)) [S].
  - AI substitutes are arriving: Fundamental XR's anatomy engine (beta), Meshy at a USD 1.5B valuation, and BioRender's 3D restyle [S].
  - The kit-business analogue is consolidating: KitBash3D was bought out in 2025 and moved to USD 708/yr unlimited plans [S].
- **Business model:** Per-kit seat licences plus studio collection licences. Sold direct and through TurboSquid, CGTrader, Fab and Superhive. Upsell custom rig adaptation.
- **Price points:** [I] All low-medium confidence except the female kit (low).
  - Seat licence: EUR 99-499 per kit.
  - Studio licence for a themed collection with dossier: EUR 1,000-3,000.
  - With custom adaptation: EUR 5,000+ (follow-up A).
  - Female pelvic kit: USD 300-3,000 (follow-up D).
- **Competitors:** TurboSquid and CGTrader rigged-anatomy sellers; DOSCH; VOKA (1,000+ models, quote); SciePro; Zygote; BioDigital (viewer only); Clarafi kits; Molecular Nodes with cellPACK (free); studio in-house libraries; AI 3D generators.
- **Founder fit:** Direct. He already owns a hyper-real female reproductive and urinary sculpt, and he is a Maya, mGear and deformer TD. He needs a urogynaecologist or scientist reviewer for each dossier. He should confirm he holds commercial rights to the sculpt; follow-up E notes a possible Atomedge link, which is unverified.
- **Evidence strength:** moderate. The supply-side gap is seen across five reports. Demand is untested.
- **Source files:** track-3, track-4, track-5, followup-A, followup-D, followup-E.

- **Price points:** USD 8-20k per 60-90 s pack (follow-up C) [I]. Anchors: VOKA from USD 7k [V]. A 60-120 s HCP MoA costs USD 15-40k (attribution uncertain) [S].
- **Competitors:** Visual Science, XVIVO, Random42, AXS3D, VOKA, Prolific, Advids, Fiverr freelancers, offshore studios (from USD 2k/min), VarsaAI, Powtoon MOA Studio, Leadde.
- **Founder fit:** Good. He can make accurate 3D from a Cape Town cost base, and GMT+2 overlaps EU hours and US East mornings. He lacks a biotech sales network and a scientific writer.
- **Evidence strength:** moderate.
- **Source files:** track-1, followup-B, followup-C.

### G4. 3D-native versioning of approved MoA masters, sold to studios and agencies

- **Kind:** service, with a later pipeline-tool licence.
- **One-liner:** Re-time from the source scene, swap in-scene 3D labels, render format and language derivatives, and export a claims-anchor list for each derivative.
- **Narrowed from:** track 2 Gap 1, track 5 Gap 3 and follow-up B. **Cut:** pharma-facing SaaS, flat-video dubbing, and any claim to shorten MLR.
- **Problem:** Approved masters spawn cut-downs, aspect ratios and language versions. Flat-video AI tools cannot edit labels inside the 3D scene or re-time animation. When a video's length changes, PromoMats anchors fall to 00:00 and someone must place them again by hand.
- **Buyer:** Small and mid-size medical-animation studios, and MedComms or healthcare agencies that hold the scene files.
- **Evidence:**
  - Video averages 32.4 days from start to approval ([Vodori](https://www.vodori.com/blog/pharma-digital-marketing-mlr-review)) [S, vendor; attribution now resolved].
  - Veeva support docs: annotations move to 00:00 when their timestamp no longer exists ([Veeva](https://support.veeva.com/hc/en-us/articles/29795579004059-How-to-Link-Anchors-to-the-Content-on-a-Video-in-PromoMats-and-Veeva-Medical)) [S, vendor docs].
  - Physicians prefer short formats ([Sermo n=499](https://sermo.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/HCP-Sentiment-Part-16-Report.pdf)) [S]. The "57%" figure is engagement data, not a survey answer.
  - Studios charge 15-20% of base price per extra language ([Prolific](https://prolificstudio.co/?p=30191)) [S, attribution unconfirmed].
  - No competitor was found doing in-scene 3D re-versioning (follow-up B) [I].
- **Counter-evidence:**
  - In Veeva's 2026 MedTech benchmark, 64% name local re-review as the main barrier ([Veeva](https://www.veeva.com/medtech/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026-MedTech-Commercial-Content-Benchmark-Report-1.pdf)) [S]. The bottleneck is review, not rendering.
  - Label changes mostly hit text overlays, not the 3D [I].
  - Source files sit with the original studio [I].
  - Flat-video localisation is a scaled commodity: Synthesia is valued at USD 4B ([link](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/26/synthesia-hits-4b-valuation-lets-employees-cash-in)) [S].
  - Big players own global-to-local adaptation: Indegene FY26 revenue rose 23.6% [C] ([link](https://financialreports.eu/filings/indegene-limited/earnings-release/2026/42183486/)), ZS ZAIDYN Content [S], IQVIA.ai [S].
  - Veeva could close the anchor gap with one feature.
- **Business model:** Per-derivative white-label fee. Later, a Maya/Blender pipeline tool sold to studios.
- **Price points:** USD 1,500-6,000 per language or format derivative of a 2-3 minute MoA (follow-up B) [I, low]. It sits between the studio's 15-20% of a USD 25-75k base and AI dubbing under USD 1,500.
- **Competitors:** Original studios; language-service providers with AI dubbing (Smartcat, Interpro, Vistatec); Synthesia, HeyGen, ElevenLabs; Indegene, ZS, IQVIA; Veeva PromoMats and Falcon MLR; Aprimo; VarsaAI.
- **Founder fit:** Strong technical fit: Maya pipeline, PySide and AI tools. Weak access: the work needs other studios' scene files.
- **Evidence strength:** weak.
- **Source files:** track-2, track-5, followup-B.

- **Business model:** Day rate or a fixed fee per procedure package.

- **Competitors:** In-house art teams, game-art outsourcers, BioDigital SDK (Anatomage), Zygote, SciePro, AI anatomy generators.
- **Founder fit:** Good on craft. Real-time rigging and anatomy fit, and contract work suits first revenue. Low growth.
- **Evidence strength:** weak.
- **Source files:** track-1, track-4, followup-A, followup-C.

### G6. Anatomy and mechanism evaluation sets and expert annotation for AI 3D and video firms

- **Kind:** service (custom builds, which can become a recurring data product).
- **One-liner:** Expert-labelled anatomy and mechanism test sets with source dossiers, expert review of model outputs, and opt-in of validated assets to enterprise data deals.
- **Narrowed from:** follow-up A's new gap and follow-up E. **Cut:** licensing a back catalogue to AI labs as training volume, and medical-imaging AI firms as buyers.
- **Problem:** AI video and 3D models make anatomy that looks right but is wrong. Most errors are in reasoning, not image quality. Data buyers increasingly order custom, tightly specified sets.
- **Buyer:** AI 3D and video firms (Meshy, Tripo, Deemos/Hyper3D, Stability). Data aggregators (Shutterstock, CGTrader enterprise). AI-labour platforms (Mercor). Possibly BioRender.
- **Evidence:**
  - Shutterstock's Data, Distribution and Services line made USD 203.3M in 2025, up 16% ([link](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/shutterstock-sstk-reports-989-9m-114655765.html)) [S]. The line is not pure AI licensing.
  - Deemos (Hyper3D) bought a commercial Shutterstock 3D licence for training on 16 Dec 2025 ([link](https://hyper3d.ai/blog/hyper3d-shutterstock)) [S].
  - CGTrader runs Custom Enterprise Deals and builds "custom assets to order" ([link](https://help.cgtrader.com/hc/en-us/articles/46191760965137-What-is-a-Custom-Enterprise-Deal-and-how-does-it-affect-my-earnings-as-a-seller)) [S].
  - Custom requests are "highly technical" ([LBB](https://lbbonline.com/news/AI-Model-Development-Training-Data-Scarcity-Shutterstock-Studios)) [S].
  - Tripo raised 3B yuan partly for 3D data infrastructure ([link](https://www.3dnatives.com/en/tripo-ai-raises-3-billion-yuan-04092026/amp/)) [S]. Meshy uses outsourced data vendors [S].
  - Mercor pays 3D artists USD 24-60/hr and expert artists USD 60-100/hr ([link](https://work.mercor.com/jobs/list_AAABlYGD8f6kNvV2OWRBPJw6/3-d-artist)) [S].
  - Accuracy problems:
    - SurgVeo: only 2.8-6.2% of Veo-3 surgical errors were image-quality errors ([link](https://the-decoder.com/googles-veo-3-can-fake-surgical-videos-but-misses-every-hint-of-medical-sense/)) [S].
    - A JMIR 2025 paper reports gross anatomy errors ([link](https://mededu.jmir.org/2025/1/e80084)) [S].
    - Ziman et al., 17 interviews ([link](https://www.theregister.com/2025/07/27/biomedviz_ai_wrong_problems/)) [C].
- **Counter-evidence:**
  - No named firm was found buying anatomical 3D data.
  - Medical AI trains on patient scans: NVIDIA MAISI used 10,277 CT volumes ([link](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.11169v1)) [S].
  - Free substitutes exist: MedShapeNet has 100k+ shapes ([link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.16139)); Objaverse [S].
  - Volume rates are tiny: USD 1-4 per minute of video, cents per image [S].
  - Revenue is lumpy. Getty booked deals early, and Shutterstock's Q4 2025 data line fell 19% [S].
  - Clean licensing is the minimum buyers expect, not a reason to pay more [I].
- **Business model:** Project fees for custom sets. Yearly subscriptions to evaluation sets. Hourly expert annotation. Revenue share through aggregators.
- **Price points:** All follow-up E inferences, low confidence, except the Mercor rates.
  - Non-exclusive licence of a 100-300 asset validated set: USD 10-75k per buyer.
  - Custom build: USD 20-150k.
  - Evaluation-set subscription: USD 5-30k a year.
  - Annotation: USD 24-100/hr (Mercor posted rates) [S].
- **Competitors:** Shutterstock/TurboSquid, CGTrader, Daz 3D, A23D, Mercor, MedShapeNet, NVIDIA MAISI, Objaverse.
- **Founder fit:** Good. It uses his AI product skills (RAG, Claude tools) and his anatomy judgement. It works as an add-on, not as the core business.
- **Evidence strength:** weak.
- **Source files:** followup-A, followup-E, track-5.

### G7. White-label 3D anatomy and MoD modules for trial-participant content providers

- **Kind:** service.
- **One-liner:** Reusable, validated 3D anatomy, disease and procedure modules supplied to Jumo-type firms and eConsent creative teams, for procedure-heavy and paediatric trials.
- **Narrowed from:** track 6 Gap 1 and follow-up C. **Cut:**
  - Selling direct to sponsors.
  - Generic "what is a trial" content: a free version exists.
  - Lay-summary text.
- **Problem:** Sponsors must explain trials in plain, translated language. The specialists keep the work in-house. Jumo is moving into a recruitment system with an AI partner, so bespoke animation becomes a part it may buy in.
- **Buyer:** Jumo-type trial-education firms. eConsent vendors' creative teams (Signant, Medable). CRO patient-recruitment groups.
- **Evidence:**
  - Fewer than half of CTIS trials reported results on time (116 of 234). Missing lay summaries were among the failures ([Transparimed](https://www.transparimed.org/single-post/half-of-clinical-trials-of-medicines-in-europe-violate-new-transparency-law-new-study)) [C].
  - ICH E6(R3) Annex 2 takes effect in the EU on 15 Jan 2027 ([EMA](https://www.ema.europa.eu/de/ich-e6-good-clinical-practice-scientific-guideline)) [C].
  - Section 1557 language access stays in force ([HHS](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/informs-covered-entities-partial-vacatur-2024-aca-nondiscrimination-final-rule-tennessee-v-kennedy.html)) [C].
  - 23 of CDER's 46 novel approvals in 2025 were orphan drugs ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/media/190705/download)) [C].
  - A kidney-biopsy RCT found that animated eConsent improved comprehension. The design is corroborated; the size of the effect was not found ([ANZCTR](https://anzctr.org.au/ACTRN12621000768897.aspx)) [C/U].
  - A 2026 meta-analysis poster: OR 2.21, but I² 87% ([PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12901548/)) [S].
  - Jumo pivot: the PRISM system with Areti AI ([link](https://www.clinicaltrialsarena.com/news/jumo-health-clinical-trial-system/)) [S]. Estimated revenue about USD 37M (aggregator, weak) [S].
- **Counter-evidence:**
  - The JAMA RCT (n=1,535) found no gain from animation, in research-cohort consent ([Duke](https://comphealth.duke.edu/publications/audiovisual-augmentation-of-electronic-consent-to-improve-consent-rates-and-comprehension-a-randomized-clinical-trial/)) [C].
  - ECRAN's free animated explainer comes in 23 languages ([link](https://en.testingtreatments.org/?p=3055)) [S].
  - No price evidence exists anywhere.
  - Signant builds in-house; Medable partners with Jumo [S].
  - Lay-summary text is being automated (TrialAssure with MMS) [S].
- **Business model:** White-label module licences plus customisation fees.
- **Price points:** None published. The only proxy is custom medical animation at USD 5-50k+ per minute (vendor guides) [V].
- **Competitors:** Jumo Health, Signant Creative Services, Medable, CISCRP, Citeline, TrialAssure, generalist studios, ECRAN (free), text-only consent.
- **Founder fit:** Medium. Anatomy and an AI translation pipeline fit. He lacks clinical-operations and IRB experience; the white-label route lowers that barrier.
- **Evidence strength:** weak.
- **Source files:** track-6, followup-C.

### G8. Hybrid: kit-backed MoA/MoD studio (G2 + G3 fund and build G1)

- **Kind:** hybrid strategy.
- **One-liner:** Sell G2 investor MoA packs and G3 women's-health device MoD for cash. Every job adds one function rig and dossier to the G1 library, which is sold on as kits.
- **Problem:** Kits alone reach only low-to-mid six figures (follow-up A). Services alone have no moat, given VOKA, Fiverr and AI-MoA tools. Together, client work pays for the library, and the library cuts the cost of each job.
- **Buyer:** Biotech CEOs and IR leads (G2). Women's-health device founders (G3). Small studios and freelancers (G1).
- **Evidence:**
  - Each component is evidenced above: G2 moderate, G3 weak, G1 moderate.
  - Follow-up C calls G2 "the most realistic first-revenue path".
  - Follow-up A calls kits "a credible first-revenue line and a portfolio and lead engine for custom rigging work".
  - Follow-up D says growth would come from "using the female pelvis as the flagship of a broader validated, function-rigged anatomy library".
  - Precedent: VOKA runs a studio and also licenses 1,000+ ready-made models ([link](https://voka.io/3d-medical-modeling-services/)) [V]. Maverick Frame reuses "one set of 3D assets across every version" [V].
- **Counter-evidence:**
  - Client contracts may assign IP to the client and block reuse. No evidence either way [I].
  - Service work can crowd out product work.
  - VOKA already runs this model, which proves it works but also occupies it.
  - The combined ceiling stays modest unless G9 works.
- **Business model:** Productised services for cash. Retain rights to generic anatomy and mechanism assets. Sell kits. Reuse cuts delivery cost.
- **Price points:** G2 USD 8-20k per pack; G3 USD 3-25k per piece; G1 EUR 99-499 per seat or EUR 1-3k per studio [I]. Follow-up D puts a small team at USD 100-300k in year 1 [I, low].
- **Competitors:** VOKA (closest), Visual Science, AXS3D, marketplace sellers.
- **Founder fit:** The best overall fit. It uses his sculpting, rigging and pipeline skills, and his AI skills can run the dossier and reference workflow. A small team can run it.
- **Evidence strength:** moderate.
- **Source files:** followup-A, followup-C, followup-D, track-1, track-3, track-4.

### G9. Hybrid: validated library as a downstream content and data layer (G1 into G5, G6, G7)

- **Kind:** hybrid strategy.
- **One-liner:** Once G1 has depth, license the same validated rigs and dossiers into three channels: AI evaluation sets and data (G6), white-label trial and patient modules (G7), and contract sim/XR work (G5).
- **Problem:** Each channel is too small or too unproven alone. One validated asset base can feed all three. This is where any larger upside sits.
- **Buyer:** AI 3D and video firms, trial-content providers, sim/XR developers.
- **Evidence:**
  - Each component is evidenced above (G5, G6 and G7 are all weak).
  - Analogues:
    - BioRender shows a validated 2D library can be worth USD 900M; it says it is profitable with 4M+ users ([link](https://www.biorender.com/blog/funding-announcement)) [C].
    - Zygote licenses its library for animation, simulation and engineering ([link](https://solidworks.com/partner-product/zygote-human-anatomy-and-skeletal-data)) [S].
    - IT'IS charges commercial fees for its Virtual Population anatomy ([link](https://itis.swiss/virtual-population/virtual-population/faq/)) [S].
- **Counter-evidence:**
  - No buyer was found in any of the three channels for artist-made anatomy.
  - Medical AI uses patient data. Sim firms and trial vendors build in-house.
  - Aggregator payouts are opaque (the TurboSquid petition) [S].
  - Revenue is lumpy.
  - It depends on G1 existing first.
- **Business model:** Non-exclusive licences, custom builds, white-label fees, and revenue share through aggregators.
- **Price points:** G6 USD 10-150k per deal; G5 USD 10-60k per package; G7 unpriced [I].
- **Competitors:** Shutterstock/TurboSquid, CGTrader, Zygote, BioDigital, SciePro, VOKA, Jumo, AI 3D generators.
- **Founder fit:** His AI skills help package the data. Enterprise sales are the gap for a small Cape Town team.
- **Evidence strength:** weak.
- **Source files:** followup-A, followup-C, followup-E, track-4, track-6.

1. **Mid-priced rigged whole-body anatomy kits** (track 4 Gap 1, broad form): rigged anatomy already sells for USD 149-2,399 [C]. Only validation and organ function survive, in G1.
2. **"BioRender of 3D": a human-authored rigged library as stated** (track 5 Gap 1, broad form): rigged libraries exist (VOKA, marketplaces). Narrowed into G1.
3. **Validated MoA-kit layer for AI-MoA tools** (track 1 Gap 3, as stated): AI-MoA vendors show no sign of buying outside assets. The studio buyers moved to G1 and the AI buyers to G6.
4. **Standalone species-level microbe library and rigged cell/microbe MoA kit** (track 4 Gap 2, track 6 Gap 2): there are cheap packs (DOSCH EUR 49 for 50; ithappy USD 69 for 190), a rigged bacteriophage, a virus-infection-cycle asset, free NIH 3D, and Molecular Nodes with cellPACK. Demand reaches microbe assets only through studios. Process rigs stay as a G1 line.
5. **Maya bio-toolkit** (track 3 Gap 2): AdonisFX 2.0/2.1 covers the soft-tissue half [C]. The molecular half rests on one undated pre-2024 page [U]. Buyers are drifting to Blender and Cinema 4D.

17. **Dental patient education** (track 6, considered): crowded, with incumbents at USD 149-267 a month.
18. **Veterinary client education** (track 6, considered): the market is mostly text, IDEXX already ships a 3D exam-room tool, and no spend data was found.
