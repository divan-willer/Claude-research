## G8. Hybrid: kit-backed MoA/MoD studio (G2 + G3 fund and build G1)

### Verdict: **survives if reframed**

As stated, a three-legged business (biotech investor MoA, women's-health device MoD, and kits) fails. Its legs do not feed each other, the founder is not a molecular-MoA animator, and the price band is already occupied. A narrow single-vertical version survives as a modest services business.

### Failure modes

1. **The flywheel is a category error** [I-high]. Investor MoA packs for Series B-C biotechs show receptor binding, antibodies, gene editing and signalling cascades. Those are molecular and cellular scenes, usually built from PDB structures or procedural tools (Molecular Nodes, cellPACK, AXS's "procedural tools to model many tissues", per the team's follow-up A). The G1 library is organ-function rigs and a female pelvis. A G2 job adds almost nothing to G1. Only G3 jobs (pelvic devices) overlap the library, and G3 is the smallest leg at USD 0.1-3M a year (follow-up D).
2. **The founder cannot deliver the cash leg alone** [I-high]. His strengths are hyper-real anatomical sculpting, creature and character rigging, and pipeline tools. A G2 pack needs molecular-biology storytelling, a scientific writer, motion design and biotech IR sales. All three scorers flagged this; they still gave founder fit 4. Selling to biotech CEOs from Cape Town with no biotech portfolio is a cold start.
3. **Informed buyers will try to take the IP that the flywheel needs.**
   - A medical-animation buying guide tells buyers to settle source-file ownership before signing. Mechanism assets get reused across congress materials, sales aids and training, and "if the studio retains the models, every downstream deliverable becomes a new negotiation" ([Shootsta](https://shootsta.com/blog/how-to-commission-medical-training-animation), snippet; attribution uncertain between this and one other guide).
   - Standard vendor clauses declare deliverables "work made for hire" with an irrevocable assignment as backstop ([Law Insider](https://lawinsider.com/clause/ownership-of-deliverables/_7), snippet; attribution uncertain).
   - The usual protection is a pre-existing-materials carve-out. In one template the vendor must tell the client in writing before using such materials, and the client gets a perpetual licence to them ([University of Arizona template](https://www.marcom.arizona.edu/sites/default/files/2024-02/Work%20Product%20Agreement_TEMPLATE_FINAL_20240215EG.pdf), snippet). UK studio guidance warns that broad "all materials" wording can sweep reusable models into the client's ownership ([Sprintlaw UK](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/key-contract-risks-for-animation-studios-in-the-uk/), snippet; attribution uncertain among Sprintlaw articles).
   - Inference [I-med]: the carve-out only protects assets that exist *before* the job. So the library must be built first, unfunded, which reverses the "client work funds the library" logic. Assets made *during* a job default to the client unless he negotiates, and negotiating usually costs price.
4. **The "transparent mid-tier price" position is taken, and the anchors sit below G8's band.**
   - Maverick Frame's self-published 2026 guide prices a sample 60-second technical explainer at about USD 8,100, quotes USD 3,000-8,000 per minute and a 4-6 week timeline, and cites a USD 45/hr studio rate ([Maverick Frame](https://maverickframe.com/lead-magnets/cases/en/show-how-it-works.pdf); [rate guide](https://maverickframe.com/lead-magnets/roadmap/de/medical-device-rendering.pdf), snippets; attribution uncertain across its guide PDFs). It also lists a "wearable bladder monitor animation" case ([Maverick Frame](https://maverickframe.com/success-stories/wearable-bladder-monitor-animation/), title only). That is a continence device: G3's niche.
   - Fiverr lists a MoA-style drug-mechanism animation at up to USD 50 with 6-day delivery ([Fiverr](https://www.fiverr.com/ola_gokeh), snippet). Sellers now advertise "AI-powered 3D medical animation" gigs ([Fiverr](https://fiverr.com/glorybright34/create-ai-powered-3d-medical-animation-anatomy-visualization-explainer-video), snippet).
   - General 3D animation in India runs about USD 300-1,000 per minute ([3DLabz](https://www.3dlabz.com/animation-price-india.html), snippet; attribution uncertain, not medical-specific).
   - The team's "no studio publishes a fixed price" (follow-up C) is now only half true. Maverick Frame publishes indicative prices at the bottom of G2's USD 8-20k band.
5. **Biotech IR firms and agencies already bundle the deck and the animation.** LifeSci Advisors, the large healthcare IR firm, has a creative director with 20+ years of biomedical graphics ([The Org](https://theorg.com/org/lifesci-advisors/org-chart/vicki-campbell), snippet). Opus Biotech Communications sells investor decks plus video ([Informa Connect](https://informaconnect.com/biotech-showcase/sponsors/opus-biotech-communications/), snippet), and SCIENT bundles investor communications with animation ([YesPress](https://yespress.io/scient), snippet). Inference [I-med]: a CEO buying a roadshow deck gets the animation from the deck vendor. An outside 3D studio has to win a channel partner, which is slower.
6. **The early-stage buyer pool is at multi-year lows.**
   - S&P Global counted 237 US venture-backed biotech rounds, down 25.2% year on year and the lowest since at least 2021 (reported March 2026) ([S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/articles/2026/3/us-biotech-rounds-of-funding-drop-to-lowest-in-5-years-99485197), snippet).
   - J.P. Morgan tracked 50 seed and Series A deals worth USD 2.3bn in Q1 2026, and first-time financings were heading for their worst year since before the pandemic ([Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/early-stage-funding-slumps-toward-post-pandemic-low-piling-more-pressure-biotech-startups), snippet).
   - GlobalData calls the recovery "hyper-selective", with money flowing to late-stage assets ([GlobalData](https://www.globaldata.com/media/pharma/biotech-funding-recovery-favors-late-stage-lower-risk-assets-reveals-globaldata/), snippet).
   - The IPO window is real but selective, and some IPO-track companies are choosing M&A instead ([BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/deals/biotechs-ipo-tide-is-finally-turning-thanks-in-part-to-the-recent-m-a-surge), snippet; attribution uncertain). Inference [I-med]: the Series B-C and newly listed buyers that do exist are well funded and go to established studios with IR references (Visual Science's Apogee, Jasper and Spyre work, from follow-up C).
7. **Deck-grade MoA is the first thing AI will take** [I-med]. Investor loops need no MLR review, which makes them the lowest-stakes MoA format.
   - An ICLR 2026 benchmark (MicroVerse/MicroWorldBench) found current top video models (Sora, Veo 3) fail at microscale biology such as cell division and DNA replication. A Wan2.1 model fine-tuned on that data gained more than 2.7 points of scientific fidelity ([arXiv 2603.00585](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00585v1), snippet). Researchers are actively closing the gap.
   - In a June 2026 test of Google's Omni Flash, 16 of 34 successful scientific clips scored at least 4/5 for accuracy ([K-Dense](https://www.k-dense.ai/blog/benchmarking-google-omni-flash-scientific-video), snippet). About half are usable after expert review.
   - Powtoon's MOA Generator already runs Veo inside a pharma workflow ([Powtoon](https://www.powtoon.com/ai/medical-affairs), snippet). Runway markets medical-animation "concept previews and lower-stakes explainers in hours" ([Runway](https://runway.com/resources/medical-animation-video), snippet). A working medical animator reports using generative AI for the cell, membrane and blood assets in a daraxonrasib MoA ([LinkedIn](https://ae.linkedin.com/in/zainab-siddiqi), snippet).
8. **The founder's flagship asset may belong to someone else** (see the Atomedge section under cross-cutting risks). G3's cost edge and G1's first SKU both rest on the female pelvic sculpt.
9. **Three go-to-market motions for one person** [I-high]: biotech IR outbound, femtech founder outbound, and a product launch, with delivery on top. The team itself names "service work crowds out product work" as a risk. It is the expected outcome at team size one or two.

## G1. Validated, function-rigged organ and cell-process kits (female pelvis first)

### Verdict: **fails**

The "organ function is improvised; only baked hearts exist" premise is contradicted by marketplace listings. That leaves a validation PDF as the only differentiator, sold through a marketplace channel that is shrinking and cutting payouts.

### Failure modes

## Cross-cutting risks (risks that hit all three)

1. **Sculpt ownership and an Atomedge conflict of interest.**
   - Atomedge says it has worked with the University of Cape Town since 2011, 3D-scanning cadavers. It claims 5,370 structures, prices by quote, offers a free demo, and says a rig is "in the process of building and testing" ([Atomedge](https://keyboard-poodle-zzyx.squarespace.com), snippet). Its FAQ lists three licence types, including a non-commercial educational licence ([Atomedge FAQ](https://keyboard-poodle-zzyx.squarespace.com/faqs), snippet). Coverage elsewhere repeats the company's own claims ([AiToolGo](https://www.aitoolgo.com/learning/detail/atomedge-3d-anatomy-model-rigged-3d-anatomy-model), snippet).
   - The founder is a Cape Town anatomy sculptor, and this session carries an Atomedge sculpt-brief skill for artists building its female anatomy model. Inference [I-med]: his female reproductive and urinary sculpt is likely Atomedge work.
   - If so: (a) the asset is probably Atomedge's IP under an employment or contractor agreement; (b) selling competing kits or using the asset in client work may breach that agreement; (c) Atomedge is the most direct competitor to G1, and a supplier G3 and G8 would depend on.
   - **This is the first thing to resolve, before any build.** It cuts both ways: Atomedge could be the partner that solves the rights, data and validation problems at once.
2. **The AI accuracy gap is closing on the cheap formats first.**
   - Fine-tuned video models are improving microscale scientific fidelity ([arXiv 2603.00585](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00585v1), snippet). About half of frontier-model scientific clips already pass expert review ([K-Dense](https://www.k-dense.ai/blog/benchmarking-google-omni-flash-scientific-video), snippet). Another benchmark finds visual quality has converged while scientific correctness still varies widely ([Sci-VBench](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2608.09873), snippet).
   - AI rigging shipped across 2026 (G5 above).
   - Studios themselves now say AI will handle "repetitive, non-creative" work ([AXS Studio](https://axs3d.com/blog/future-of-biotech-animation-2026/), snippet).
   - The "No AI" protection is eroding: commentary argues blanket clauses are unworkable ([The Contract Network](https://thecontractnetwork.com/the-end-of-no-ai-clauses-in-clinical-research-agreements/), snippet), and life-sciences deals increasingly allow enterprise-licensed AI ([National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/data-ownership-life-sciences-transactions-why-it-matters-more-ever-age-ai), snippet).
   - In the UK, 46% of illustrators now know a client chose generative AI over them, up from 32% a year earlier ([Resultsense on AOI 2026](https://www.resultsense.com/news/2026-11-04-aoi-illustrators-generative-ai-survey-2026/), snippet).
3. **Labour supply glut.** Game layoffs, the Pixomondo shutdown, No.8's insolvency, offshore studios at about USD 300-1,000 a minute and Upwork anatomy work at USD 10-45/hr all push down every service price in G5 and G8 (sources above).
4. **Zero buyer interviews.** Across 11 research files and this red team, not one buyer has been asked whether they would pay for any of the three offers. Every price point in G1, G5 and G8 is inference or vendor list price. This is the single largest evidence gap.
5. **Platform dependence.** Kits depend on marketplaces that are cutting payouts (CGTrader 55-80%), licensing sellers' assets for AI training (TurboSquid, per follow-up A), and flooding with AI uploads. Fab pays 88% but its whole creator pool earned about USD 24M in 2025.
6. **Incumbent consolidation means fewer, bigger buyers with in-house teams.** Anatomage owns BioDigital, Cengage owns Visible Body, Norstella owns Primal and Citeline (follow-up C). Each has clinicians and artists on staff.
7. **Location and cash flow** (minor). GMT+2 suits EU hours and US East mornings. Eskom projects no load-shedding for winter 2026 ([Eskom](https://www.eskom.co.za/stable-power-system-performance-reinforced-by-operational-and-cost-efficiencies-winter-outlook-projecting-no-loadshedding/), snippet), but its medium-term outlook shows a high likelihood of load-shedding in 2029-30 ([Daily Investor](https://dailyinvestor.com/energy/118224/eskom-warns-of-high-likelihood-of-load-shedding-in-the-future/), snippet). Large life-sciences buyers pay on terms of 60 days or more: GSK gives small US suppliers 60+5 days only on request ([GSK](https://dev-supplier.gsk.com/PolicyGuide), snippet), and large firms increasingly demand net 60-120 ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=2699438), snippet). For a one- or two-person studio, a USD 15k invoice paid 90 days late is a real problem.
8. **No path to "massive growth" in any of the three.** Even before my attacks, the team's own ceilings were USD 100-300k in year 1 (G8), hours times rate (G5) and EUR 50-300k a year by years 2-3 (G1). If the founder's goal really is massive growth, none of the top 3 meets it on this evidence. Any upside sits in an untested data or platform play (G9, scored 16.9 with weak evidence) or outside this study.
