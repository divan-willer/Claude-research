### What changed and why

- G8 Pelvic-health device MoD studio: procedure and mode-of-delivery animation for pelvic-floor, continence, prolapse and obstetric-injury device firms, plus pelvic anatomy and rig subcontracting to generalist studios (G2 dropped): 20.7 → 17.8. Renamed to the red team's single-vertical version. With G2 gone, market drops 2→1 (pelvic device MoD is about USD 0.1-3M/yr) and growth falls to G3's 2. I rejected the WTP cut because USD 3-8k jobs still score 3, and the G2-only cuts to fit and competition no longer apply. Atomedge rights and no femtech network put TTR at 3.5; rigs built in advance and licensed lift defensibility to 2.3.
- G6 Anatomy and mechanism evaluation sets and expert annotation for AI 3D and video firms: 17.9 → 17.3. Not attacked. Two shared findings apply: free academic scientific-video benchmarks (MicroWorldBench, Sci-VBench) and laid-off artists filling Mercor-type pools. They cut competition 2→1.7 and defensibility 2.3→2. Growth stays at 3.3 because K-Dense says every AI clip needs expert review, and fine-tuning on curated data raised fidelity.
- G3 MoD and procedure animation for women's-health, pelvic-floor and continence device firms: 19 → 16.7. Not attacked, but the G8 evidence hits it too. Maverick Frame's continence case and published price of about USD 8.1k per 60 s, plus VOKA's pelvic stock animation, put competition at 2. Work-for-hire clauses cut defensibility to 1.7. The Atomedge risk and no femtech network give fit 4.0 and TTR 3. Reframed G8 replaces it.
- G5 Contract anatomy and rig art for sim/XR developers and medtech education teams: 19 → 16. Accepted competition 1 (layoff glut, USD 29.99/hr agency, AI auto-rigging) and defensibility 1. Partly accepted market 1.3, growth 1.5 (Mentice device-maker orders +36% in H1 2026 offset the XR decline) and WTP 2.5 (USD 3.6-6k/month contracts exist). Not renamed: bridge income only.
- G4 3D-native versioning of approved MoA masters, sold to studios and agencies: 15 → 14.8. Not attacked. The labour glut and AI MoA and versioning tools (Powtoon, VarsaAI) trim competition 1.7→1.5. Buyer advice to own the source files may ease access to scene files, but that is unproven, so no score goes up.
- G2 Productised 3D investor MoA packs for Series B-C and newly listed biotechs: 19 → 14.5. The red team's G8 evidence hits this gap directly. Maverick Frame at about USD 8.1k per 60 s, Fiverr and IR-firm bundling give competition 1 and WTP 3. 237 US VC rounds (fewest since 2021) cut growth to 2. Gaps in molecular craft and biotech IR sales give fit 2.5. No portfolio means TTR 3. AI takes deck-grade MoA first, so defensibility is 1.
- G1 Validated, function-rigged organ and cell-process kits (female pelvis first): 19 → 14.3. Accepted the direction of all five cuts but softened four. Competition is 1.5 because the listings are snippet-level and rig quality is unverified. WTP is 1.5 because confirmed USD 149-2,399 rigged-anatomy sales exist. Growth is 1.3 because the CGTrader 'correction' is partly revenue concentrating in top models. Fit is 4.3 because only the asset is at risk, not his skill. The rest becomes the showcase asset in G8.
- G9 Hybrid: validated library as a downstream content and data layer (G1 into G5, G6, G7): 16.9 → 14.3. Depends on G1, whose premise failed. Defensibility 3.3→2.3: incumbents claim expert review and dossiers can be copied. Competition 1.5: Atomedge already pitches its model for AI training. Market 2 and growth 2.5: the G5 XR channel is shrinking. Fit 2.7 for the sculpt-rights risk.
- G7 White-label 3D anatomy and MoD modules for trial-participant content providers: 13.7 → 13.4. Not attacked. AI tools that make 'lower-stakes explainers in hours' (Runway) and automated lay summaries cut defensibility 2→1.7. There is still no price evidence.

### Number one

G8: #1 is reframed G8: a pelvic-health device MoD studio that also does pelvic anatomy and rig subcontracting for generalist studios. It has the highest revised total (17.8, against 17.3 for G6 and 16.7 for G3). It is also the only option that combines three things:
- A realistic first paid deal in 6-12 months: a subcontract for a generalist studio such as Maverick Frame or Advids, or a USD 3-8k device MoD job priced against Maverick Frame and VOKA.
- Work squarely in his strengths: hyper-real pelvic anatomy and Maya function rigs.
- An asset that keeps its value: pelvic function rigs built before any job and licensed to clients, not assigned, which gets around the work-for-hire IP problem.

It fails the 'massive growth' half. The pool is USD 0.1-3M a year and the ceiling is about USD 100-300k a year, a lifestyle studio. No gap in this study clears that bar on the evidence. G6 has the best growth score but no named buyer and lumpy deals. G9's platform upside collapsed with G1's premise.

So G8 is the cash engine and the growth option is G6, tested in parallel: expert-validated pelvic and anatomy evaluation sets, plus expert review for AI 3D and video firms. It reuses the same validated assets and his Claude and RAG skills. It is not #1 because buyers are unproven.

Gating steps, in order:
1. Settle the sculpt rights. Atomedge may own the sculpt (unverified); settle as owner, licensee or partner.
2. Run 10 buyer interviews with device founders and studio owners.
3. Build the pelvic function rigs as pre-existing materials before the first job.
4. Fund the gap with part-time contract roles, which is what is left of G5, as bridge income only.

G3 is now part of #1. G2 and G1 drop as standalone plays.
