## Summary

**Top-down reports disagree by about 16x.** 2025 "medical animation market" estimates include $512M at 19.3% CAGR ([Straits](https://straitsresearch.com/report/medical-animation-market)), $577M at 20.6% ([Fortune BI](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/medical-animation-market-116796)), $0.61B at 22.5% ([TBRC via GII](https://www.gii.tw/report/tbrc1717221-medical-animation-global-market-report.html)) and $3.9B ([Reanin](https://www.reanin.com/reports/medical-animation-market)) (all snippet). These are paywalled report summaries: weak evidence.

**The events that trigger animation spend are flat, not growing 20% a year.** CDER approved 55 novel drugs in 2023, 50 in 2024 and 46 in 2025 ([SCI](https://soci.org/news/2026/1/the-fda-approved-these-46-new-drugs-in-2025), [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/novel-drug-approvals-fda/novel-drug-approvals-2025), snippet). CBER added 12 novel biologics in 2025, 5 of them cell or gene therapies ([Prime Therapeutics](https://www.primetherapeutics.com/e/3dcc7e87-0ffb-febb-5977-9e238f153f67/20132/11103214), snippet). EMA recommended 104 medicines in 2025, 38 with a new active substance ([EMA](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/news/emas-2025-annual-report-shows-strong-approval-numbers-human-veterinary-medicines), snippet). FDA cleared about 3,200 510(k)s in 2025 and granted 27 De Novos, down from 47 in each of 2023 and 2024 ([Pure Global](https://www.pureglobal.com/blog-posts/fda-cleared-vs-approved-vs-granted-510k-pma-de-novo), snippet).

Inference (confidence: medium): the 19-23% CAGRs in market reports do not show up in occasion counts. Launch-linked animation spend more likely grows in low single digits, unless the number of pieces per launch rises.

Inference (confidence: low): a bottom-up build from occasion counts and vendor list prices gives about $25M-$260M a year for launch-, device- and fundraising-triggered animation (arithmetic in the table notes). That is below the $0.5-0.6B report cluster. The difference is probably patient education, hospital libraries and education software that reports fold in.

**Demand signals are mixed.** Down: the average healthcare marketing budget fell 21% to $7.2M in 2024 ([eMarketer on MM+M/Swoop](https://www.emarketer.com/content/healthcare-marketing-budgets-tumbled-last-year), snippet). Healthcare venture investment hit $60B over 2,167 deals in 2025, up from $45.4B ([HSBC via Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260113190645/en), snippet). There were at least 31 biotech IPOs by 26 October 2026, against 8-11 in all of 2025 ([BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/business/biotech-notches-31-ipos-already-this-year-with-4-more-joining-queue), snippet).

**Supply looks like surplus, not shortage.** An offshore studio advertises 3D MoA from $2,000 per minute in 2-4 weeks ([Chasing Illusions](https://www.chasingillusions.com/3d-moa-animations/), snippet). ## Top 3 candidate gaps

### Gap 1: Rigged, deformable anatomy content for surgical/procedural simulation and device training

**Problem.** Simulation vendors, device makers and nursing sim labs need accurate soft-tissue anatomy that deforms believably. Simulation is the segment with the most consistent growth forecasts, and education programs are short of faculty and clinical placements.

**Evidence**
- Four firms put 2025 surgical simulation at $176M-$720M with 14.7%-16.7% CAGR: MarketsandMarkets $176.0M to $349.4M by 2030 ([Digital Media Net](https://digitalmedianet.com/?p=83469)); Grand View $530.73M, 16.57% ([Research and Markets](https://www.researchandmarkets.com/report/surgical-simulation)); Persistence $720.3M, 16.7% ([Persistence](https://www.persistencemarketresearch.com/market-research/surgical-simulation-market.asp)); Knowledge Sourcing $402.28M to $852.234M by 2030, 16.20% ([MarketResearch.com](https://www.marketresearch.com/Knowledge-Sourcing-Intelligence-LLP-v4221/Surgical-Simulation-Forecasts-42727044)). All snippet, all weak sources.
- Broader medical simulation: $2.058B in 2025 to $3.847B in 2030, 13.33% ([Knowledge Sourcing](https://www.knowledge-sourcing.com/report/global-medical-simulation-market)); $2.7B in 2025, 11.0% ([Persistence](https://www.persistencemarketresearch.com/market-research/medical-simulation-market.asp)). Snippet.
- FDA granted 27 De Novos and 38 original PMAs in 2025: novel devices that need surgeon training ([Pure Global](https://www.pureglobal.com/blog-posts/fda-cleared-vs-approved-vs-granted-510k-pma-de-novo), snippet).
- AACN: 1,588 vacant full-time faculty posts (7.2% of 21,993) across 863 schools; about 92,672 qualified applications turned away; schools cite too few clinical placement sites ([AACN fact sheet](https://www.aacnnursing.org/news-information/fact-sheets/nursing-faculty-shortage), snippet).

**Who pays.** Product managers and CTOs at surgical/VR simulation companies; professional-education directors at medtech companies; nursing simulation lab directors; education publishers.

**Rough price points.** Evidence thin. I found no list or deal prices for licensed sim anatomy content. Parent-market sizes above are not capturable spend.

**Competitors / substitutes.** In-house art teams at sim vendors; Visible Body (now Cengage); general 3D outsourcing studios. Not researched in depth on this track.

**Founder fit.** Strong: hyper-real anatomical sculpting plus Maya rigging and custom deformers is the core skill this needs.

**Confidence.** Low-medium (growth signal medium; capturable dollars unknown).

### Gap 2: Fast, fixed-price MoA/MoD packages for venture-backed biotechs around financings and IPOs

**Problem.** Biotechs raising money need accurate MoA visuals for decks, roadshows and partnering on short timelines. The 2026 IPO window reopened while large health agencies are consolidating.

**Evidence**
- Healthcare venture investment $60B over 2,167 deals in 2025, up from $45.4B in 2024; rounds of $100M+ were about 43% ([Business Wire, HSBC](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260113190645/en), snippet).
- Biotech IPOs: 8 in 2025 by BioSpace's count, 11 by BioPharma Dive's; at least 31 by 26 Oct 2026 ([BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/business/biotech-notches-31-ipos-already-this-year-with-4-more-joining-queue), [BioPharma Dive](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/biotech-ipo-performance-q1-2026/815879/), snippet). Sleuth counts 43 drug-developer IPOs worldwide raising ~$9.1B through 24 Sept 2026 ([Sleuth](https://sleuthinsights.com/analysis/2026-pharma-biotech-ipos-by-month), snippet).
- Cyclical risk: HSBC data showed quarterly biotech venture funding falling from $7B to $4.8B in Q2 2025, and first financings from $2.6B to $0.9B ([BioPharma Dive](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/biotech-venture-capital-funding-h1-2025-hsbc/753283/), snippet).
- Omnicom closed its IPG takeover with 4,000 layoffs; health agency creatives and strategists were among those cut ([Fierce Pharma](https://www.fiercepharma.com/marketing/amid-close-omnicom-interpublic-megamerger-dana-maiman-named-ceo-health-arm), [Campaign](https://campaignlive.com/article/omnicom-health-hasnt-reorganized-its-ad-agencies-yet/1941937), snippet).

**Who pays.** Biotech CEOs, CFOs, heads of investor relations and corporate communications, chief business officers; VC platform teams.

**Rough price points.** Vendor list prices, 2025-26: from $2,000/min offshore ([Chasing Illusions](https://www.chasingillusions.com/3d-moa-animations/)); $4,500-$15,000 per finished minute ([Motifmotion](https://motifmotion.com/services/scientific-animation)). Higher ranges ($15k-$35k/min; $25k-$75k for a 2-3 minute MoA film) appeared on [North Animation](https://northanimation.com/3d-medical-animation-cost) or [Animated Videos](https://animatedvideos.co/blog/medical-animation-complete-guide/); I could not tell which. No deal prices found. All snippet.

**Competitors / substitutes.** Specialist studios (XVIVO, Ghost Medical, Visual Science); offshore studios; AI MoA tools (VarsaAI); in-house designers using stock assets.

**Founder fit.** Good: accurate MoA work with an AI-assisted pipeline from a Cape Town cost base, GMT+2 overlapping EU and US East mornings.

**Confidence.** Medium on 2026 demand direction; low on durability.

### Gap 3: Validated, review-ready asset and "MoA kit" layer for AI-MoA tools and studios

**Problem.** Prices per minute are being pushed down by offshore studios and AI tools. The accuracy and MLR review step, not rendering, sets the timeline. Whoever supplies pre-validated, reusable assets and claim-linked scenes could cut review cycles for many producers.

**Evidence**
- Studios say timelines run 4-12 weeks, with review and approvals as the long pole ([Moonbeam](https://www.moonb.io/industries/medical-animation), [Visual Science](https://visual-science.com/services/medical-animation), snippet).
- AI MoA vendors exist and pitch speed: VarsaAI "12 minutes, not 12 weeks" ([VarsaAI](https://studio.varsaai.com/blog/biotech-content-marketing), snippet).
- AI content in pharma hit accuracy and compliance problems ([Indegene](https://www.indegene.com/what-we-think/reports/generative-ai-life-sciences-lessons-path-forward), snippet).
- Pfizer's "Charlie" AI content platform, built with Publicis, launched in Feb 2024 to support content creation and legal review ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/marketing/with-charlie-pfizer-is-building-a-new-generative-ai-platform-for-pharma-marketing), [afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/digital/pfizer-introduces-charlie-a-new-ai-platform-for-pharma-marketing), snippet). Digiday cites McKinsey saying gen AI could halve content creation costs.
- A Reuters Events piece claims pharma spends over $2B a year on content that never reaches an audience and argues for modular, pre-referenced content ([Reuters Events](https://events.reutersevents.com/pharma/article/optimize-content-operations), snippet).

**Who pays.** Studio owners and technical directors; product leads at AI-MoA platforms; production heads at medcomms agencies; pharma content-operations leads.

**Rough price points.** Evidence thin. This track found no asset-library or licensing prices.

**Competitors / substitutes.** Studios' private libraries; AI tools' own asset sets; publisher-owned 3D libraries (Visible Body/Cengage). Stock 3D marketplaces not researched here.

**Founder fit.** Very strong: sculpting, rigging, pipeline tools and AI products in one person.

**Confidence.** Low (demand inferred from deflation signals, not measured).
