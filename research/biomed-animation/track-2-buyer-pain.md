# Track 2: Buyer pain

**Method note (read first).** WebSearch worked for about 31 queries. Then the team's shared search budget (200 per turn) ran out. WebFetch failed with DNS errors on the first two hosts I tried (www.vodori.com and pantheon.veeva.com), and the tool told me to stop trying other sites. Those hosts would need to be allowed in the session's network settings. So I opened no pages. Every citation below is "(snippet)": it comes from search-result summaries, not from pages I read. Many numbers come from vendor marketing pages, and those vendors have a reason to quote them. A fact-checker with page access should verify the load-bearing claims first.

## Summary

**Cost.** Published prices for medical animation vary by more than 10x, and almost all of them come from studios. One studio's guide gives USD 1,000–60,000 per finished minute, with the "middle of the market" at USD 8,000–25,000 and surgical/procedural work at USD 15,000–40,000+ (list prices, 2025–26) [source](https://prolificstudio.co/blog/medical-device-animation-cost/) (snippet). Voka notes that one studio calls USD 5,000/min "industry standard" and another says the same of USD 35,000/min [source](https://voka.io/medical-animation-cost/) (snippet). A comparison page from an AI-video vendor puts custom work at USD 5,000–50,000+/min and Ghost Productions at USD 15K–100K+ per video [source](https://knowlify.com/articles/best-medical-healthcare-animation-agencies) (snippet). I found no deal prices and no independent survey. Evidence is thin on price by type (MoA vs MoD vs device vs patient education).

**Turnaround.** Studio pages quote 2–16 weeks for an MoA film. Typical figures are 6–10 weeks. A 1.5–2 min film can stretch to 12–16 weeks when there are extra review rounds or multi-market localisation, and one guide says MLR, not animation, is usually the bottleneck [source](https://axs3d.com/services/moa-mechanism-action-animation/), [source](https://cdistudio.io/services/mechanism-of-action-animation), [source](https://motifmotion.com/services/scientific-animation) (snippet; the search summary did not say which page gave which number). A directory lists Random42 at 14–16 weeks on average, including med-legal review [source](https://voka.io/best-pharma-video-production-companies/) (snippet).

**MLR.** Vodori's 2025 benchmark: average job duration 14.8 days (13.8 in 2024), average review duration 7.6 days (6.6 in 2024), 1.5 circulations [source](https://www.vodori.com/blog/how-your-medical-legal-regulatory-review-compares-in-2025) (snippet). One search summary credits a Vodori page with saying video takes longest, at 32.4 days, against 4.9 days for speaker decks [source](https://www.vodori.com/blog/pharma-digital-marketing-mlr-review) (snippet). A second summary found this figure only on a third-party page that labels it "days to approval" [source](https://contentrevops.com/resources/life-science-social-media-and-linkedin-marketing) (snippet). Veeva says MLR review holds at about 21 days, while pre-review work adds 5–150 days per asset [source](https://www.veeva.com/eu/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Veeva-PromoMats-Next-gen-MLR-Review.pdf?4) (snippet). EY's 2024 benchmark: 24 days to approval [source](https://idx.klick.com/articles/from-checkpoint-to-input-a-different-model-for-mlr) (snippet). Veeva launched AI agents for PromoMats in 2025 (a "Quick Check" agent that screens content before MLR), and Moderna went live first [source](https://www.veeva.com/customer-stories/modernas-7-step-guide-to-veeva-ai-for-promomats/) (snippet).

**Short form.** In Sermo's February 2025 survey (n=499 physicians), short text and video were the preferred formats, and only 26% wanted long-form reports [source](https://sermo.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/HCP-Sentiment-Part-16-Report.pdf) (snippet). Sermo's 2025 guide: 57% prefer videos under 5 minutes [source](https://s.sermo.com/rs/239-PZX-356/images/Sermo_Guide%20to%20Smarter%20HCP%20Marketing%202025.pdf) (snippet). Sermo sells HCP media, so it has an interest in this result.

**Localisation.** Vendor estimates: USD 2,500–6,000 per language for a 10-minute patient video with traditional dubbing [source](https://videodubbing.com/blog/post/healthcare-video-localization-complete-guide/) (snippet). USD 25K–60K to dub a 5-minute video into 10 languages, which AI dubbing cuts by 60–90% [source](https://videodubbing.com/blog/post/clinical-trial-recruitment-video-localization/) (snippet). I found no data on how many markets a global launch covers or on what share of assets get reused.

**"No AI" clauses.** I found **no documentary evidence** of "No AI" clauses in pharma, MedComms or medical-animation contracts. What does exist: generic sample clauses that bar vendor AI use without "prior written consent" [source](https://www.lawinsider.com/clause/use-of-artificial-intelligence) (snippet), and EU AI Act labelling duties, as one agency reads them [source](https://www.spectrumscience.com/perspectives/critical-implications-of-eu-ai-act-for-healthcare-communications-what-it-means-for-pharma/) (snippet). The US Copyright Office (29 Jan 2025) said prompts alone do not make someone an author [source](https://ipwatchdog.com/2025/01/29/part-two-copyright-office-ai-report-says-creative-prompting-doesnt-constitute-authorship/) (snippet). The opposite evidence is stronger. Pfizer built "Charlie" with Publicis for content creation and legal review [source](https://bestmediainfo.com/2024/02/pfizer-launches-genai-platform-charlie-in-collaboration-with-publicis-groupe) (snippet). Pharma procurement is asking whether it "should pay less" when AI speeds agency work [source](https://www.campaignlive.com/article/procurement-leaders-want-agencies-2026/1965219) (snippet). Powtoon's AI "MOA Studio" claims MoA visuals "in a matter of hours" [source](https://briefglance.com/articles/ai-tool-slashes-pharma-video-production-from-weeks-to-hours) (snippet). Accuracy risk is documented: an interview study of 17 practitioners is titled "It looks sexy but it's wrong" [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.14494v2) (snippet).

**Supply side.** I found no evidence of a studio capacity shortage. Instead, 46% of 549 illustrators say they lost work to genAI (32% in 2025), and 31% say clients expect lower fees [source](https://www.resultsense.com/news/2026-11-04-aoi-illustrators-generative-ai-survey-2026/) (snippet). That survey covers illustrators in general, not medical ones. Agencies are consolidating: Omnicom began 4,000 layoffs after the IPG deal, and health creatives were among those cut [source](https://www.mmm-online.com/news/omnicom-health-hasnt-reorganized-its-ad-agencies-yet/) (snippet).

**Universities.** Anatomage tables cost about USD 85K–140K in public quotes [source](https://www.unf.edu/procurement/files/Certified.so.AnatomagePriceQuotation.pdf) (snippet). Complete Anatomy prices institutional licences case by case; the individual student licence is USD 39.99/yr [source](https://www.elsevier.support/complete-anatomy/answer/how-does-pricing-work) (snippet). Schools that move off cadavers cite cost and ethics [source](https://www.statnews.com/2025/03/31/medical-school-cadaver-lab-body-donation-technology-substitute/) (snippet). I found no direct complaints about licence costs or faculty time, so evidence is thin.

Inference (confidence: medium): buyers' sharpest measurable pain is time to an approved, versioned video, not the cost of the first master. Video seems to be the slowest content type in MLR. HCPs want short cuts. Each language version adds cost. Procurement now expects AI-driven savings.

Inference (confidence: medium): the "No AI" framing in H2 is not supported. The real position is that buyers want AI speed but need accurate, defensible, human-authored outputs that can be disclosed. That favours AI-*assisted* pipelines with provenance records over pure generative video.

## Top 3 candidate gaps

### Gap 1: Fast, MLR-friendly "versioning" of 3D MoA/MoD masters (short-form cuts, aspect ratios, languages, indication updates)

**Problem.** A pharma or biotech brand pays for one 2–3 min 3D master. Then it needs 15–60 s HCP cut-downs, vertical social versions, versions per market and language, and updates when the label or indication changes. Each version goes back through a slow video MLR path and costs extra at the studio. The work is mostly re-rendering, re-timing and swapping on-screen text, which a pipeline can automate.

**Evidence**
- Video is reported as the slowest content type in promotional review, at 32.4 days, against 4.9 for speaker decks ([Vodori](https://www.vodori.com/blog/pharma-digital-marketing-mlr-review), snippet; disputed, see below).
- Average MLR job duration rose from 13.8 to 14.8 days between 2024 and 2025 ([Vodori 2025](https://www.vodori.com/blog/how-your-medical-legal-regulatory-review-compares-in-2025), snippet).
- Veeva: review takes about 21 days, and pre-review adds 5–150 days per asset ([Veeva PDF, May 2025](https://www.veeva.com/eu/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Veeva-PromoMats-Next-gen-MLR-Review.pdf?4), snippet).
- 57% of HCPs prefer videos under 5 min ([Sermo 2025](https://s.sermo.com/rs/239-PZX-356/images/Sermo_Guide%20to%20Smarter%20HCP%20Marketing%202025.pdf), snippet). Only 26% want long-form ([Sermo HCP Sentiment 16](https://sermo.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/HCP-Sentiment-Part-16-Report.pdf), snippet).
- Studio FAQs say extra review rounds and multi-market localisation push a 6–8 week MoA to 12–16 weeks ([AXS](https://axs3d.com/services/moa-mechanism-action-animation/), [CDI](https://cdistudio.io/services/mechanism-of-action-animation), snippet; page-to-figure mapping unverified).
- Procurement is asking whether pharma "should be paying less" when AI makes agency work faster, and pricing models are "still being hammered out" ([Campaign/MM+M 2026](https://www.campaignlive.com/article/procurement-leaders-want-agencies-2026/1965219), snippet).
- Indegene describes moving video "from finished product to modular narratives", with rules applied earlier as genAI video tools connect to MLR systems ([Indegene](https://insights.indegene.com/what-we-think/blogs/elevating-pharma-engagement-with-genai-video-marketing), snippet).

**Who pays.** Brand directors and associate brand managers (US/EU pharma and biotech), omnichannel/content-operations leads, medical affairs content leads, and agency producers at healthcare agencies and MedComms shops that subcontract 3D work.

**Rough price points.** No direct benchmark for a "versioning" service exists. The anchors are: master animation USD 8,000–25,000 per minute in the mid-market (list, 2025–26, [Prolific](https://prolificstudio.co/blog/medical-device-animation-cost/), snippet); traditional dubbing USD 2,500–6,000 per language per 10-min video (list, vendor, [videodubbing.com](https://videodubbing.com/blog/post/healthcare-video-localization-complete-guide/), snippet). Inference (confidence: low): a versioning retainer of USD 1,000–5,000 per derivative, or a per-brand annual fee, would undercut new studio work and still give a small team healthy margins.

**Competitors / substitutes.** The original studio (re-cuts at its own rates). Large content-ops outsourcers: Indegene's revenue rose from about INR 28,393m (FY25) to about INR 35,105m (FY26) ([Arihant](https://www.arihantcapital.com/company-information/directors-report/43575), [NSE filing](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/ixbrl/INTEGRATED_FILING_INDAS_154410_29042026203316_iXBRL_WEB.html), snippet). Publicis Sapient Bodhi claims 75% faster production and up to 45% lower cost ([Publicis Sapient](https://knowledge.publicissapient.com/17/stories-empowering-healthcare-marketing-with-genai-qcea.html), snippet, vendor claim). AI dubbing vendors. Powtoon Visual Pharma.

**Founder fit.** Strong: Maya rigging, pipeline/PySide tooling and AI products are exactly what an automated re-render and versioning pipeline needs.

**Confidence.** Medium on the pain; low on willingness to pay a *new* vendor rather than the original studio.

### Gap 2: Reference-annotated, MLR-ready 3D scene modules for MoA/MoD (licensed to agencies and studios)

**Problem.** Agencies rebuild cells, organs and pathways, then defend each visual element to MLR reviewers. Pre-review work is the longest part of the cycle. A library of scientifically sourced 3D modules, each with its own reference dossier (citation per structure, scale and colour conventions, version history), could cut both build time and MLR queries.

**Evidence**
- Pre-review adds 5–150 days per asset, while MLR itself holds at about 21 days ([Veeva](https://www.veeva.com/eu/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Veeva-PromoMats-Next-gen-MLR-Review.pdf?4), snippet). Veeva MedTech's version says rising content volume has not changed MLR cycle times ([Veeva MedTech PDF, Jan 2026](https://www.veeva.com/medtech/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/MedTech_PromoMats-Next-gen-MLR-Review.pdf), snippet).
- Under modular content, MLR approves modules for future reuse as well as finished pieces ([Pulse Digital](https://pulsedigital.co.uk/veeva-modular-content), snippet). Module approval is itself "lengthy" ([Reuters Events](https://www.reutersevents.com/pharma/node/63059), snippet).
- Modular-content adopters have "yet to fully reap" the benefits, and volumes fall short of business cases ([Campana & Schott](https://www.campana-schott.com/us/company/media-events/detail/content-strategy-in-pharma-ai-leads-the-way-1), snippet). This is a warning as well as a pain signal.
- GenAI visuals look plausible but are often scientifically wrong ([arXiv 2507.14494](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.14494v2), [The Register 27 Jul 2025](https://www.theregister.com/2025/07/27/biomedviz_ai_wrong_problems/), snippet). Validated, human-authored assets therefore keep value.
- Prompt-only AI output is not copyrightable in the US ([IPWatchdog, 29 Jan 2025](https://ipwatchdog.com/2025/01/29/part-two-copyright-office-ai-report-says-creative-prompting-doesnt-constitute-authorship/), snippet). Buyers that need to own the IP benefit from human-authored assets.

**Who pays.** Heads of production and 3D leads at MedComms and healthcare agencies; small medical-animation studios; in-house creative teams at pharma and device companies; biotech communications leads (investor and partnering decks).

**Rough price points.** No benchmark found for licensed, reference-annotated 3D medical modules. Nucleus licenses its library on 1/3/5-year terms, custom-quoted ([Knowlify](https://knowlify.com/articles/best-medical-healthcare-animation-agencies), snippet). Evidence thin.

**Competitors / substitutes.** Nucleus Medical Media library; general 3D marketplaces; studios' internal asset libraries; Anatomage and Elsevier's Complete Anatomy (education, not commercial licensing); genAI image tools.

**Founder fit.** Strong: hyper-realistic anatomical sculpting plus rigging is the core asset-building skill. A RAG-backed reference dossier per asset uses his AI skills.

**Confidence.** Low–medium. The pain is inferred from MLR and modular-content data, not from buyers asking for this. H1 is another track's job, so its evidence should be merged in.

### Gap 3: Multilingual, short-form 3D patient-education content for patient-support programmes and health systems

**Problem.** Patient-support programmes, hospitals and device makers need explanations of procedures and diseases in many languages and on phones. Today they license generic libraries or pay per-language dubbing on custom video.

**Evidence**
- Traditional dubbing costs USD 2,500–6,000 per language per 10-min patient video, and a 20-video, 5-language library costs USD 250K–600K before revisions (vendor, [videodubbing.com](https://videodubbing.com/blog/post/healthcare-video-localization-complete-guide/), snippet).
- Patients with non-English language preference use video visits much less often ([JAMA Netw Open 2024, DOI 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2024.57477](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/challenges-to-video-visits-for-patients-with-nonenglish-language-preference), snippet). This is about live visits, not education videos.
- The NHS commissions patient-education animation, for example four 3-min videos for Thames Valley Cancer Alliance in Jan 2025 ([Kino Bino](https://www.kinobino.com/animation/healthcare-nhs-guide-for-cancer-patients), snippet).
- A Northern Ireland government animation framework was worth GBP 170,000 over four years, Sept 2025–Aug 2029 ([Educational Voice](https://educationalvoice.co.uk/government-animation-tender/), snippet). This is not health-specific.

**Who pays.** Patient-services and patient-support-programme leads at pharma companies; patient-education managers and nurse educators at health systems; marketing at device companies; NHS trusts and cancer alliances (through tenders).

**Rough price points.** No hospital licence prices were found for Emmi, Krames or Healthwise ([Avia marketplace](https://resources.marketplace.aviahealth.com/top-patient-education-companies/), snippet). Evidence thin.

**Competitors / substitutes.** Emmi (Wolters Kluwer), Krames/WebMD Ignite, Healthwise, Nucleus, ViewMedica, and AI video and dubbing tools (Knowlify, HeyGen and others).

**Founder fit.** Medium: anatomical realism is a plus (his female reproductive/urinary work suits urology, gynaecology and continence patient education), but this is a crowded, sales-heavy channel.

**Confidence.** Low.

## Where sources disagree

- **MLR cycle time.** Vodori 2025 gives a 14.8-day job and a 7.6-day review. Veeva gives about 21 days for review. EY 2024 gives 24 days to approval. Vodori pages also give a pharma median review of 3.9 days and a median job of 6.9 days (search summary). Vodori's 2026 report is said to show a 10.7-day average review across industries. The clocks are defined differently (job vs review vs approval; mean vs median), so they cannot be compared directly ([Vodori](https://www.vodori.com/blog/how-your-medical-legal-regulatory-review-compares-in-2025), [Veeva](https://www.veeva.com/eu/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Veeva-PromoMats-Next-gen-MLR-Review.pdf?4), [Klick/EY](https://idx.klick.com/articles/from-checkpoint-to-input-a-different-model-for-mlr), all snippet).
- **Video at 32.4 days.** One search credited it to Vodori. Another found it only on a third-party page labelled "days to approval", under a heading that says "review duration".
- **Price per minute.** The spread is about 60x (USD 1,000 to 60,000+ per minute). Voka notes studios call both USD 5,000 and USD 35,000 "industry standard" ([Voka](https://voka.io/medical-animation-cost/), snippet). Knowlify and Voka also sell competing services.
- **MoA turnaround.** From 15–25 days (30–60 s piece) up to 14–16 weeks (Random42 average incl. med-legal) ([Voka directory](https://voka.io/best-pharma-video-production-companies/), snippet).
- **AI and illustrator demand.** The AOI survey shows rising work loss. A careers site rates medical illustration as high AI risk ([CareerHud](https://careerhud.com/careers/medical_illustrator), snippet). Studios and academics argue AI cannot meet accuracy needs ([Lupine Studios](https://lupinestudios.com/why-scientific-illustrators-will-not-be-replaced-by-ai/), [U of T BMC](https://bmc.med.utoronto.ca/news-events/2025/3/4/ai-in-mi-catastropportunity), snippet).
