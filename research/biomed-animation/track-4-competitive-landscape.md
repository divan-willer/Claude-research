## Summary

**1. Anatomy-atlas platforms have consolidated into publishers and hardware firms, and deal prices were not disclosed.**
- Anatomage (the virtual dissection table maker) bought BioDigital. The deal was announced on 10 Jan 2025 and its terms were not disclosed [BioSpace/PR Newswire](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/anatomage-announces-acquisition-of-cloud-based-software-company-biodigital) (snippet). BioDigital had raised only about $4.68M in total [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/biodigital/financials) (snippet).
- Cengage Group completed its purchase of Visible Body on 13 Jan 2025. The sellers were Capitala Group and Lineage Capital, and the terms were not disclosed [MergerLinks](https://app.mergerlinks.com/transactions/2025-01-13-visible-body) (snippet).
- Complete Anatomy has belonged to Elsevier since 2019, bought for about €46m / $50m [EdSurge](https://edsurge.com/news/2019-11-27-elsevier-acquires-3d-anatomy-content-provider-for-50m) (snippet) [pre-2024, possibly stale].
- Primal Pictures (Anatomy.tv) moved from Informa to the Warburg Pincus-backed Citeline/Norstella group in 2022 [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primal_Pictures) (snippet).

Inference (confidence: medium): the 3D anatomy atlas segment is mature. A new atlas app would compete with Elsevier, Cengage and Anatomage. It would also compete with freemium VOKA, which charges US $65/yr for PRO [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1554827597) (snippet) and reports 100,000 monthly active users [EdTech Innovation Hub](https://edtechinnovationhub.com/news/voka-says-its-3d-anatomy-pathology-app-now-has-100000-active-monthly-users-as-demand-for-interactive-models-grows) (snippet).

**2. BioRender is the one breakout.**
- An investment valued BioRender at $900M and gave employees liquidity. Sidley, the law firm that advised on it, announced it in Jan 2026 [Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/newslanding/newsannouncements/2026/01/sidley-represents-biorender-in-investment-valuing-company-at-%24900-million) (snippet).
- Its last widely reported primary round was a $15M Series A in Apr 2023 [Seedtable](https://seedtable.com/companies/biorender/funding-rounds/series-a-2023-04) (snippet) [pre-2024, possibly stale].
- Revenue is not public.
- A third-party site, dated Sep 2026, lists the Industry Individual plan at $95/month billed annually [ConceptViz](https://conceptviz.app/blog/biorender-pricing-2026) (snippet).

Inference (confidence: high): researchers and companies pay recurring seat fees for a validated, licensed, easy-to-use library of scientific visuals. Inference (confidence: medium): I found no 3D or animation-grade equivalent of BioRender.

**3. Private-equity money goes into MedComms agency groups, not into animation tools.**
- THL bought Red Nucleus in Oct 2024 for a reported price above $750M [Axios](https://www.axios.com/pro/health-tech-deals/2024/10/17/thl-buys-red-nucleus-for-over-750m) (snippet).
- Lockwood, which is backed by Ares, bought the London animation studio Random42 in June 2022. The price was not disclosed, and the seller (Graphite Capital) made 3.1x its cost [Graphite Capital](https://www.graphitecapital.com/news/graphite-capital-sells-random42-to-the-lockwood-group) (snippet) [pre-2024, possibly stale].
- Smaller agency deals in 2024 and 2025: Bioscript–Enzyme, JPA–BioCentric, JPA–akt, Minds + Assembly–BOLDSCIENCE and MJH–BPD (see the acquisitions table).
- Inizio Medical announced a partnership with Synthesia for AI video creation [Inizio](https://inizio.com/insights/inizio-medical-partners-with-synthesia-to-unlock-ai-powered-video-creation-for-healthcare-at-scale/) (snippet; date not shown).

Inference (confidence: medium): the biggest buyers of pharma animation are agency groups. They are consolidating and testing AI video. That cuts against the "No AI" part of H2.

**4. Immersive surgical training is stalling.** Osso VR raised more than $100M, including a $66M Series C in 2022. It changed CEO in Feb 2024 and filed a WARN notice for 67 staff in Mar/Apr 2024. MedTech Dive reported that "art and illustration roles [were] particularly badly affected" [MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/osso-vr-lay-off-67-people-san-francisco/711814/) (snippet).

**5. Patient education is consolidated.** WebMD bought Healthwise's operating assets in Feb 2024, adding them to Krames, which it got in 2020. WebMD Ignite now serves more than 650 organisations, including over 50% of US hospitals [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/webmd-picks-healthwise-build-out-patient-engagement-solutions-expand-its-footprint-650) (snippet).

**6. H1: there is a price gap between cheap marketplace models and enterprise licences.**
- **Cheap end.** Zygote models on TurboSquid cost $99–$300 [TurboSquid](https://www.turbosquid.com/3d-model/enhanced-licenses/zygote) (snippet). Some medical collections there are licensed for "editorial uses only" [TurboSquid](https://www.turbosquid.com/3d-models/3d-medical-human-body-1331644) (snippet). CGTrader sells rigged hearts made by independent sellers, with no validation [CGTrader](https://www.cgtrader.com/3d-models/human-heart) (snippet). Fab sells a game-style pack of 190 low-poly microorganism models with 38 animations [Fab](https://www.fab.com/listings/274fe874-b248-44ef-8f73-84deef0f41c7?lang=en) (snippet).
- **Expensive end.**
  - SciePro licenses one male or female model from €29,900 and its full collection from €49,900. The licence is perpetual [SciePro pricing](https://www.sciepro.com/pricing) (snippet). It covers 3,600+ structures for commercial use [SciePro](https://www.sciepro.com/3d-models) (snippet). Its library can be filtered for rigging, but the cell models I sampled were unrigged [SciePro dendritic cell](https://www.sciepro.com/library/3d-models/dendritic-cell-1709668702088) (snippet).
  - Zygote sells its collections by quote only. Rigging is offered only as a male add-on, for Maya only [Zygote](https://www.zygote.com/poly-models/3d-human-collections/3d-premier-anatomy-collection) (snippet).
- **Molecular layer.** Clarafi charges $29.99–$49.99/month for membership. The mMaya plugin is free to members, and kits cost extra [Clarafi](https://clarafi.com/membership/) (snippet).

Inference (confidence: medium): there is a missing middle. Nobody I found sells validated, rigged organ or cell kits that open in Maya, Blender or Houdini, at about €300–€5,000 with a commercial licence. The counter-evidence is in the hypothesis table. Long-established studios such as Random42 (founded 1992) and Blausen (1991) own their own libraries, and stock clips sell for $525–$1,250 each.

**Top gaps, in order of promise:**
1. Mid-priced, validated, rigged anatomy kits for animation studios and agency in-house teams.
2. A rigged cell and microbe kit for MoA (mechanism-of-action) animation, in offline and real-time versions.
3. Rigged, deformable, real-time anatomy supplied as an outsourced service to surgical and nursing simulation and XR developers.

## Top 3 candidate gaps

### Gap 1: Mid-priced, validated, rigged anatomy kits that open in Maya, Blender or Houdini, for medical animation studios and agency in-house teams

**Problem.** A studio that needs an anatomically accurate, rigged organ system (for example the female pelvic, reproductive and urinary system) has two kinds of option.
- A $99–$300 marketplace model. It has no validation, and some carry editorial-only licences.
- A five-figure enterprise licence from SciePro or Zygote, most of it unrigged.
